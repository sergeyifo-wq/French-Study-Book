## Learning Objectives
- Learn vocabulary for travel and transportation
- Ask for and give directions

## Dialogue: Planning a Trip

**Context**: Sophie and Marie are planning a weekend trip to Lyon.

---

**Sophie**: Marie, tu veux aller quelque part ce week-end?
**Marie**: Oui, j'aimerais bien! Où est-ce que tu veux aller?

**Sophie**: J'ai entendu dire que Lyon est très beau. On pourrait y aller.
**Marie**: Bonne idée! Comment est-ce qu'on y va?

**Sophie**: On peut prendre le train. Il y a un TGV direct de Paris à Lyon.
**Marie**: Combien de temps est-ce que ça prend?

**Sophie**: Environ deux heures. C'est rapide!
**Marie**: Parfait! À quelle heure est-ce que le train part?

**Sophie**: Il y a un train à 8 heures le samedi matin. Ça te va?
**Marie**: Oui, c'est parfait. Et où est-ce qu'on dort?

**Sophie**: J'ai trouvé un hôtel près de la gare. Il coûte 80 euros par nuit.
**Marie**: C'est raisonnable. On réserve?

**Sophie**: Oui, je vais réserver les billets de train et l'hôtel.
**Marie**: Super! Je suis excitée!

## Dialogue: At the Train Station

**Context**: Sophie and Marie are at the train station buying tickets.

---

**Marie**: Bonjour! Deux billets pour Lyon, s'il vous plaît.
**Employé**: Aller simple ou aller-retour?

**Sophie**: Aller-retour, s'il vous plaît.
**Employé**: Pour quel jour?

**Marie**: Pour samedi, le 15 octobre.
**Employé**: À quelle heure?

**Sophie**: Le train de 8 heures, s'il vous plaît.
**Employé**: Très bien. Vous voulez des places ensemble?

**Marie**: Oui, s'il vous plaît. C'est combien?
**Employé**: Ça fait 120 euros pour deux.

**Sophie**: Voici 120 euros.
**Employé**: Merci! Voici vos billets. Le train part de la voie 5.

**Marie**: À quelle heure est-ce qu'il arrive à Lyon?
**Employé**: À 10 heures.

**Sophie**: Merci beaucoup!
**Employé**: De rien! Bon voyage!

## Grammar: Near Future with "Aller"

### Forming the Near Future

The near future (futur proche) is used to express actions that will happen in the near future. It's formed with:

**aller (present tense) + infinitive verb**

| Subject | Aller | Infinitive | English |
|---------|-------|------------|---------|
| Je | vais | voyager | I'm going to travel |
| Tu | vas | prendre le train | You're going to take the train |
| Il/Elle/On | va | partir | He/She/One is going to leave |
| Nous | allons | arriver | We're going to arrive |
| Vous | allez | réserver | You're going to reserve |
| Ils/Elles | vont | visiter | They're going to visit |

**Examples:**
- Je **vais** **prendre** le train. (I'm going to take the train.)
- Tu **vas** **aller** à Paris? (Are you going to go to Paris?)
- Il **va** **partir** demain. (He's going to leave tomorrow.)
- Nous **allons** **visiter** le musée. (We're going to visit the museum.)
- Vous **allez** **réserver** une chambre? (Are you going to reserve a room?)
- Ils **vont** **arriver** à 8 heures. (They're going to arrive at 8 o'clock.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Negative Form**: "ne...pas" surrounds "aller"
   - Je **ne vais pas** voyager ce week-end. (I'm not going to travel this weekend.)
   - Il **ne va pas** prendre l'avion. (He's not going to take the airplane.)

2. **Question Form**: Invert subject and "aller"
   - **Vas**-tu prendre le train? (Are you going to take the train?)
   - **Va**-t-il partir demain? (Is he going to leave tomorrow?)

3. **Common Time Expressions**:
   - demain (tomorrow)
   - ce week-end (this weekend)
   - la semaine prochaine (next week)
   - dans une heure (in one hour)
   - plus tard (later)
   - bientôt (soon)

## Grammar: Passé Composé with "Avoir"

### Forming the Passé Composé

The passé composé is used to express actions that happened in the past. For most verbs, it's formed with:

**avoir (present tense) + past participle**

| Subject | Avoir | Past Participle | English |
|---------|-------|-----------------|---------|
| J' | ai | pris | I took |
| Tu | as | pris | You took |
| Il/Elle/On | a | pris | He/She/One took |
| Nous | avons | pris | We took |
| Vous | avez | pris | You took |
| Ils/Elles | ont | pris | They took |

**Examples:**
- J'**ai** **pris** le train. (I took the train.)
- Tu **as** **vu** le film. (You saw the movie.)
- Il **a** **mangé** au restaurant. (He ate at the restaurant.)
- Nous **avons** **visité** le musée. (We visited the museum.)
- Vous **avez** **réservé** une chambre. (You reserved a room.)
- Ils **ont** **attendu** le bus. (They waited for the bus.)

### Past Participles of Common Verbs

| Infinitive | Past Participle | English |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|
| prendre | pris | to take |
| voir | vu | to see |
| manger | mangé | to eat |
| boire | bu | to drink |
| faire | fait | to do/make |
| aller | allé | to go |
| arriver | arrivé | to arrive |
| partir | parti | to leave |
| finir | fini | to finish |
| attendre | attendu | to wait |
| réserver | réservé | to reserve |
| visiter | visité | to visit |
| acheter | acheté | to buy |
| payer | payé | to pay |

### Important Notes:

1. **Agreement**: With "avoir", the past participle does NOT agree with the subject. It stays the same.
   - J'ai mangé une pomme. (I ate an apple.)
   - Nous avons visité Paris. (We visited Paris.)

2. **Negative Form**: "ne...pas" surrounds "avoir"
   - Je **n'ai pas** pris le train. (I didn't take the train.)
   - Il **n'a pas** vu le film. (He didn't see the movie.)

3. **Question Form**: Invert subject and "avoir"
   - **As**-tu pris le train? (Did you take the train?)
   - **A**-t-il mangé? (Did he eat?)

## Cultural Note: Travel in France

Travel is an important part of French culture, and France has an excellent transportation infrastructure:

**Rail Travel**:
- France has one of the best rail networks in the world, operated by SNCF (Société Nationale des Chemins de fer Français).
- **TGV (Train à Grande Vitesse)**: The high-speed train that connects major cities. TGVs can reach speeds of up to 320 km/h (200 mph).
- **TER (Transport Express Régional)**: Regional trains that connect smaller towns and cities.
- **Intercités**: Trains that connect cities without being high-speed.
- **Métro**: Subway systems in major cities like Paris, Lyon, Marseille, and others.
- **Tramway**: Tram systems in many cities.

**Road Travel**:
- France has an extensive network of highways (autoroutes).
- Many French people own cars, but in cities, public transportation is often preferred.
- **Péage**: Tolls on highways, which can be expensive but ensure good road conditions.
- **Aires de repos**: Rest areas along highways with facilities.
- **Covoiturage**: Carpooling, which is becoming increasingly popular.
- **Location de voitures**: Car rental, available at airports and train stations.

**Air Travel**:
- France has many airports, with Paris having three major ones: Charles de Gaulle, Orly, and Beauvais.
- Other major airports include Lyon-Saint Exupéry, Nice Côte d'Azur, Marseille Provence, and others.
- **Air France** is the national airline.
- Budget airlines like EasyJet and Ryanair also operate in France.

**Urban Transportation**:
- **Métro**: Subway systems are efficient and cover most major cities.
- **Bus**: Bus networks complement the metro systems.
- **Tramway**: Tram systems are being developed in many cities.
- **Vélib'**: Bike-sharing systems in many cities, especially Paris.
- **Taxis**: Available but can be expensive. Ride-sharing apps like Uber also operate.

**Travel Culture**:
- The French love to travel, both within France and abroad.
- **Week-end**: Short weekend trips are very popular. The French often take advantage of the excellent rail network to visit other cities.
- **Vacances**: Longer vacations, especially in the summer. Many French people have second homes in vacation areas.
- **Congés payés**: Paid vacation time, with most employees getting at least 5 weeks per year.
- **RTT (Réduction du Temps de Travail)**: Additional days off for employees who work more than 35 hours per week.

**Popular Destinations**:
- **Paris**: The capital city with world-famous landmarks like the Eiffel Tower, Louvre, and Notre-Dame.
- **Provence**: Known for its lavender fields, vineyards, and charming villages.
- **The French Riviera (Côte d'Azur)**: Famous for its beaches, resorts, and cities like Nice and Cannes.
- **The Loire Valley**: Known for its beautiful châteaux (castles).
- **Brittany**: Known for its rugged coastline, Celtic heritage, and seafood.
- **Normandy**: Known for its history (D-Day beaches), apple orchards, and dairy products.
- **The Alps**: Popular for skiing and hiking.
- **The Pyrenees**: Mountain range on the border with Spain, popular for hiking and skiing.

**Travel Tips**:
- **Rail Passes**: Consider rail passes like the France Rail Pass for extensive train travel.
- **Reservations**: Always make reservations for TGVs and popular trains.
- **Validation**: Validate your train ticket before boarding (composter votre billet).
- **Luggage**: There's no luggage limit on trains, but be considerate of other passengers.
- **Punctuality**: French trains are generally very punctual. Arrive at the station a few minutes early.
- **Strikes**: Be aware that strikes (grèves) can affect public transportation. Check for updates before traveling.

**Accommodation**:
- **Hôtels**: Range from luxury to budget options.
- **Chambres d'hôtes**: Bed and breakfasts, often in private homes.
- **Gîtes**: Self-catering vacation rentals, often in rural areas.
- **Auberges de jeunesse**: Youth hostels.
- **Camping**: Camping is popular, especially in natural areas.
- **Airbnb**: Increasingly popular for short-term rentals.

**Dining While Traveling**:
- **Bouchons**: Traditional restaurants in Lyon.
- **Brasseries**: Brewpub-style restaurants, often serving local dishes.
- **Bistros**: Small, casual restaurants.
- **Cafés**: Often serve light meals and drinks.
- **Marchés**: Markets where you can buy fresh, local products.
- **Pique-niques**: Picnics are a great way to enjoy local food while traveling.
