# A2 Unit 06: Time and Dates

## Learning Objectives
- Tell time in French with precision
- Talk about dates (days, months, years)
- Use ordinal numbers
- Talk about schedules and appointments
- Use time expressions and adverbs
- Understand French date and time formats

## Dialogue: Making an Appointment

**Context**: Sophie is calling to make a doctor's appointment.

---

**Sophie**: Bonjour, je voudrais prendre un rendez-vous avec le docteur Martin, s'il vous plaît.
**Secrétaire**: Bonjour! Bien sûr. Pour quand?

**Sophie**: Je voudrais un rendez-vous la semaine prochaine, si possible.
**Secrétaire**: La semaine prochaine... Nous avons des créneaux le lundi, le mercredi et le vendredi.

**Sophie**: Je préfère le mercredi. À quelle heure?
**Secrétaire**: Le mercredi, nous avons un rendez-vous à 10 heures et un autre à 15 heures.

**Sophie**: Je prends celui de 10 heures, alors.
**Secrétaire**: Très bien. Le mercredi 15 novembre à 10 heures.

**Sophie**: Parfait! Et combien de temps dure le rendez-vous?
**Secrétaire**: Environ 30 minutes.

**Sophie**: D'accord. Je dois apporter quelque chose?
**Secrétaire**: Non, juste votre carte Vitale et votre carte d'identité.

**Sophie**: Très bien. Merci beaucoup!
**Secrétaire**: De rien! À mercredi!
**Sophie**: À mercredi!

## Dialogue: Talking About Schedules

**Context**: Marie and Sophie are discussing their weekly schedules.

---

**Marie**: Sophie, tu as des projets pour ce week-end?
**Sophie**: Oui, je vais au cinéma samedi soir. Et toi?

**Marie**: Moi, je travaille samedi matin. Mais samedi après-midi, je vais faire du shopping.
**Sophie**: Super! On pourrait aller ensemble?

**Marie**: Avec plaisir! À quelle heure est-ce que tu veux y aller?
**Sophie**: Vers 14 heures?

**Marie**: Parfait! Et dimanche, qu'est-ce que tu fais?
**Sophie**: Dimanche, je vais chez mes parents. Et toi?

**Marie**: Moi, je vais me reposer. J'ai une semaine chargée devant moi.
**Sophie**: Moi aussi! J'ai beaucoup de travail cette semaine.

**Marie**: Tu as des réunions?
**Sophie**: Oui, j'ai une réunion lundi à 9 heures, une autre mercredi à 14 heures, et une dernière vendredi à 16 heures.

**Marie**: Oh là là! Tu vas être très occupée!
**Sophie**: Oui, mais je vais gérer.

## Grammar: Telling Time

### Basic Time Telling

To tell time in French, use:

**Il est + hour + minutes**

**Examples:**
- **Il est** trois heures. (It's 3:00.)
- **Il est** trois heures et quart. (It's 3:15.)
- **Il est** trois heures et demie. (It's 3:30.)
- **Il est** quatre heures moins le quart. (It's 3:45.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Hour Agreement**: The hour number agrees in gender with "heure" (feminine).
   - **Il est** deux heures. (It's 2:00.) - "deux" (masculine) agrees with "heure" (feminine)
   - **Il est** trois heures. (It's 3:00.) - "trois" (masculine) agrees with "heure" (feminine)

2. **Minutes**: For minutes past the hour, use "et" + number.
   - **Il est** trois heures **et** dix. (It's 3:10.)
   - **Il est** trois heures **et** vingt. (It's 3:20.)

3. **Minutes to the Hour**: For minutes to the next hour, use "moins" + number.
   - **Il est** quatre heures **moins** dix. (It's 3:50.)
   - **Il est** quatre heures **moins** vingt. (It's 3:40.)

4. **Quarter Hours**:
   - **et quart** = quarter past (15 minutes)
   - **et demie** = half past (30 minutes)
   - **moins le quart** = quarter to (45 minutes)

5. **Noon and Midnight**:
   - **Il est** midi. (It's noon.)
   - **Il est** minuit. (It's midnight.)

**Examples:**

- **À quelle heure** est le rendez-vous? (At what time is the appointment?)

## Grammar: Dates

### Writing Dates

In French, dates are typically written in the format: **day month year**

**Examples:**
- 15 juillet 2024 (July 15, 2024)
- 25 décembre 2023 (December 25, 2023)
- 1er janvier 2025 (January 1, 2025)

### Saying Dates

To say dates in French, use:

**Le + ordinal number + month + year**

**Examples:**
- **Le** premier **juillet** 2024. (July 1st, 2024.)
- **Le** deux **mai** 2023. (May 2nd, 2023.)
- **Le** quinze **août** 2025. (August 15th, 2025.)

### Important Notes:

1. **First of the Month**: Use "le premier" for the 1st of the month.
   - **Le premier** janvier (January 1st)

2. **Other Days**: Use the ordinal number (deuxième, troisième, etc.) or the cardinal number (deux, trois, etc.).
   - **Le deux** mai (May 2nd) - more common
   - **Le deuxième** mai (May 2nd) - also correct but less common

3. **Months**: Month names are not capitalized in French.
   - **le** 15 **juillet** 2024 (not "Juillet")

4. **Years**: Years are pronounced as cardinal numbers.
   - 2024 = deux mille vingt-quatre (/duh meel vahn-ka-treh/)
   - 2000 = deux mille (/duh meel/)
   - 1999 = mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix-neuf (/meel nuhf son ka-truh-vahn dee-nuhf/)

### Asking About Dates

| French | English | Pronunciation |
|--------|---------|---------------|

**Examples:**

## Grammar: Ordinal Numbers

### Forming Ordinal Numbers

Most ordinal numbers are formed by adding the suffix **-ième** to the cardinal number:

| Cardinal | Ordinal | English | Pronunciation |
|----------|---------|---------|---------------|
| un | premier/première | 1st | /preh-myehr/ |
| deux | deuxième | 2nd | /duh-zhee-em/ |
| trois | troisième | 3rd | /trwah-zyem/ |
| quatre | quatrième | 4th | /ka-tyehr-m/ |
| cinq | cinquième | 5th | /sank-zyem/ |
| six | sixième | 6th | /see-zyem/ |
| sept | septième | 7th | /set-zyem/ |
| huit | huitième | 8th | /wee-tyem/ |
| neuf | neuvième | 9th | /nuh-vyehm/ |
| dix | dixième | 10th | /dee-zyem/ |
| onze | onzième | 11th | /on-zyem/ |
| douze | douzième | 12th | /doo-zyem/ |
| vingt | vingtième | 20th | /vahn-tyem/ |
| vingt-et-un | vingt-et-unième | 21st | /vahn-tay-oon-yem/ |
| trente | trentième | 30th | /tron-tyem/ |
| cent | centième | 100th | /son-tyem/ |

### Important Notes:

1. **First**: "premier" (masculine) and "première" (feminine) are irregular.
   - **Le premier** jour (the first day)
   - **La première** fois (the first time)

2. **Second**: "deuxième" is the most common form, but "second" is also used, especially for rankings.
   - **Le deuxième** jour (the second day)
   - **Le second** étage (the second floor)

3. **Agreement**: Ordinal numbers agree in gender and number with the noun they describe.
   - **Le troisième** jour (the third day - masculine)
   - **La troisième** fois (the third time - feminine)
   - **Les troisième** jours (the third days - masculine plural)
   - **Les troisième** fois (the third times - feminine plural)

4. **Plural**: The ordinal number itself does not change in the plural, but the article and noun do.
   - **Le premier** jour (the first day)
   - **Les premiers** jours (the first days)

## Grammar: Talking About Schedules

### Present Tense for Schedules

Use the present tense to talk about scheduled events in the future:

**Examples:**
- Le train **part** à 8 heures. (The train leaves at 8 o'clock.)
- Le film **commence** à 20 heures. (The movie starts at 8 PM.)
- Le rendez-vous **est** à 15 heures. (The appointment is at 3 PM.)

### Common Schedule Phrases

| French | English | Pronunciation |
|--------|---------|---------------|

| ...à... | ...at... | /a/ |
| ...le... | ...on... | /luh/ |
| ...avec... | ...with... | /a-vek/ |
| ...pour... | ...for... | /poor/ |
| C'est... | It's... | /say/ |
| C'est à... | It's at... | /say a/ |
| C'est le... | It's on... | /say luh/ |
| C'est avec... | It's with... | /say a-vek/ |

**Examples:**
- **J'ai un rendez-vous** à 10 heures **le** lundi. (I have an appointment at 10 o'clock on Monday.)
- **C'est** à 15 heures **le** mercredi. (It's at 3 o'clock on Wednesday.)
- **J'ai un rendez-vous** avec le médecin **à** 9 heures. (I have an appointment with the doctor at 9 o'clock.)

## Cultural Note: Time and Dates in France

Understanding how the French handle time and dates is important for both daily life and cultural understanding:

**Time Format**:
- France uses the **24-hour clock** (military time) in most formal contexts, including:
  - Transportation schedules (trains, buses, planes)
  - Business hours
  - Appointments
  - Official documents
- However, in **conversation**, the 12-hour clock is often used, especially for times before noon.
  - 8:00 = huit heures (8 o'clock)
  - 15:00 = trois heures (3 o'clock) or quinze heures (15:00)
  - 20:00 = huit heures (8 o'clock) or vingt heures (20:00)

**Date Format**:
- France uses the **day-month-year** format for dates.
  - 15/07/2024 = 15 juillet 2024 (July 15, 2024)
  - 01/01/2024 = 1er janvier 2024 (January 1, 2024)
- This is different from the **month-day-year** format used in the United States.

**Punctuation**:
- In written dates, the French use:
  - **Spaces** or **slashes** for numeric dates: 15/07/2024 or 15 07 2024
  - **No commas** in numbers: 1 000 (not 1,000)
  - **Commas** for decimal points: 3,5 (not 3.5)

**Time Zones**:
- Metropolitan France uses **Central European Time (CET, UTC+1)**.
- During **Daylight Saving Time** (from last Sunday in March to last Sunday in October), France uses **Central European Summer Time (CEST, UTC+2)**.
- France's overseas territories have different time zones:
  - **French Guiana**: UTC-3
  - **Guadeloupe and Martinique**: UTC-4
  - **Réunion and Mayotte**: UTC+4
  - **French Polynesia**: UTC-10

**Public Holidays**:
- France has **11 official public holidays** (jours fériés), during which most businesses and schools are closed:
  - **1er janvier**: New Year's Day
  - **Lundi de Pâques**: Easter Monday
  - **1er mai**: Labor Day
  - **8 mai**: Victory in Europe Day
  - **Ascension**: Ascension Day (40 days after Easter)
  - **Lundi de Pentecôte**: Whit Monday
  - **14 juillet**: Bastille Day
  - **15 août**: Assumption Day
  - **1er novembre**: All Saints' Day
  - **11 novembre**: Armistice Day
  - **25 décembre**: Christmas Day
- Note that **Easter Sunday** and **Christmas Eve** are not official public holidays, but many businesses may close early or have reduced hours.

**School Holidays**:
- France has a **three-zone system** for school holidays, with different regions having slightly different schedules.
- The school year typically runs from **early September to early July**, with holidays:
  - **Toussaint** (All Saints' Day): 2 weeks in late October/early November
  - **Noël** (Christmas): 2 weeks in late December/early January
  - **Hiver** (Winter): 2 weeks in February/March
  - **Printemps** (Spring): 2 weeks in April
  - **Été** (Summer): 8 weeks in July/August

**Business Hours**:
- **Shops**: Typically open from **9:00 or 10:00 AM to 7:00 or 8:00 PM**, with a long lunch break (often from 12:00 to 2:00 PM).
- **Banks**: Typically open from **9:00 AM to 5:00 PM**, with a lunch break.
- **Restaurants**: Lunch service is typically from **12:00 to 2:00 PM**, and dinner service from **7:00 to 10:00 PM**.
- **Offices**: Typically open from **9:00 AM to 6:00 PM**, with a lunch break.
- **Sunday**: Most shops are closed, except for some bakeries, newsstands, and shops in tourist areas.

**Lunch Break**:
- The **long lunch break** (pause déjeuner) is a characteristic of French work culture.
- Many businesses close for **1-2 hours** during lunch (typically from 12:00 to 2:00 PM).
- This is a time for employees to enjoy a proper meal and relax.
- In some smaller towns, shops may close for the entire afternoon.

**Punctuality**:
- The French generally value punctuality, especially for **business meetings and appointments**.
- However, there is often more flexibility for **social events** and **dinner parties**, where arriving 15-30 minutes late is not uncommon.
- For **trains and public transportation**, punctuality is expected, and arrivals/departures are usually on time.

**Appointments**:
- When making appointments (rendez-vous), it's important to:
  - **Confirm the date and time** clearly.
  - **Arrive on time** or inform the other person if you're running late.
  - **Bring any necessary documents** (ID, insurance card, etc.).
  - **Cancel in advance** if you can't make it.
- Common types of appointments include:
  - **Médical**: Doctor, dentist, specialist
  - **Administratif**: Government offices, banks
  - **Professionnel**: Business meetings, job interviews
  - **Personnel**: Hairdresser, beauty salon

**Time Expressions**:
- The French use many time-related expressions:
  - **"À la bonne heure!"**: At the right time! / Just in time!
  - **"Il est l'heure!"**: It's time! / Time's up!
  - **"Il est temps de..."**: It's time to...
  - **"Il est trop tôt."**: It's too early.
  - **"Il est trop tard."**: It's too late.
  - **"Dans le temps"**: In the past / In the old days
  - **"De mon temps"**: In my time / When I was young
  - **"En temps et lieu"**: In due time and place
  - **"En temps normal"**: Normally / Under normal circumstances

**Historical Time**:
- France has a rich history, and the French often refer to historical periods:
  - **La Préhistoire**: Prehistory
  - **L'Antiquité**: Antiquity
  - **Le Moyen Âge**: The Middle Ages
  - **La Renaissance**: The Renaissance
  - **Les Temps Modernes**: Modern Times
  - **La Révolution française**: The French Revolution (1789)
  - **Le Premier Empire**: The First Empire (Napoleon, 1804-1814)
  - **La Troisième République**: The Third Republic (1870-1940)
  - **Les Années Folles**: The Roaring Twenties (1920s)
  - **La Seconde Guerre mondiale**: World War II (1939-1945)
  - **Les Trente Glorieuses**: The Thirty Glorious Years (1945-1975, post-war economic boom)

**Time in French Culture**:
- The French have a **different relationship with time** than some other cultures:
  - **Quality over speed**: The French often prioritize enjoying the moment over rushing.
  - **Meals**: Meals are seen as a time to relax and enjoy good food and company, not to be rushed.
  - **Conversation**: The French often engage in long conversations, whether with friends, family, or even strangers.
  - **Work-life balance**: The French value their free time and have a strong work-life balance.
  - **"La dolce vita"**: The French approach to life emphasizes enjoying the good things in life.

**Time-Related Proverbs**:
- The French have many proverbs related to time:
  - **"Le temps, c'est de l'argent."** (Time is money.)
  - **"Tout vient à point à qui sait attendre."** (All things come to those who wait.)
  - **"Il n'est jamais trop tard pour bien faire."** (It's never too late to do well.)
  - **"Le temps guérit tout."** (Time heals all.)
  - **"Un tiens vaut mieux que deux tu l'auras."** (A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush - literally: One you have is worth more than two you will have.)
  - **"Qui vivra verra."** (Time will tell - literally: Whoever lives will see.)
  - **"Le temps passe et n'efface aucune ride."** (Time passes and erases no wrinkles - meaning: Some things never change.)
