# A2 Unit 04: Directions and Places

## Learning Objectives
- Learn vocabulary for places in a city
- Ask for and give directions
- Understand and use prepositions of place
- Describe locations and landmarks
- Use imperative form for giving directions
- Talk about distances and travel times

## Dialogue: Asking for Directions

**Context**: Sophie is lost in Lyon and asks a passerby for directions.

---

**Sophie**: Excusez-moi, monsieur! Je suis perdue. Pouvez-vous m'aider?
**Passant**: Bien sûr! Où est-ce que vous voulez aller?

**Sophie**: Je cherche la Basilique Notre-Dame de Fourvière. Comment est-ce que j'y vais?
**Passant**: C'est un peu loin à pied. Vous pouvez prendre le métro.

**Sophie**: Le métro? Où est la station la plus proche?
**Passant**: La station la plus proche est juste là, à 100 mètres. C'est la station "Vieux Lyon".

**Sophie**: Et quelle ligne est-ce que je prends?
**Passant**: Prenez la ligne D, direction "Gare de Vénissieux". Descendez à "Fourvière".

**Sophie**: Combien de temps est-ce que ça prend?
**Passant**: Environ 10 minutes. Ensuite, c'est à 5 minutes à pied de la station.

**Sophie**: Merci beaucoup! Et pour revenir, je fais la même chose?
**Passant**: Oui, exactement. Prenez la ligne D dans l'autre direction.

**Sophie**: Merci! Vous êtes très gentil.
**Passant**: De rien! Bonne visite!

## Dialogue: Giving Directions

**Context**: Marie gives Sophie directions to a restaurant.

---

**Marie**: Sophie, tu veux aller au restaurant "Le Bouchon Lyonnais"?
**Sophie**: Oui, j'ai très faim! Comment est-ce que j'y vais?

**Marie**: C'est très simple. Sors de l'hôtel et tourne à gauche.
**Sophie**: D'accord. Et après?

**Marie**: Marche tout droit pendant environ 5 minutes. Tu vas passer devant une église.
**Sophie**: Une église?

**Marie**: Oui, l'église Saint-Georges. Après l'église, tourne à droite.
**Sophie**: Et ensuite?

**Marie**: Ensuite, marche tout droit pendant 2 minutes. Le restaurant est à gauche, juste après le café "Le Comptoir".
**Sophie**: C'est loin?

**Marie**: Non, c'est à environ 10 minutes à pied de l'hôtel.
**Sophie**: Parfait! Merci!

## Grammar: Giving Directions

### Imperative Form

The imperative is used to give directions. It's the same as the present tense, but without the subject pronoun.

| Pronoun | Present Tense | Imperative | English |
|---------|---------------|------------|---------|
| tu | tu tournes | tourne | turn |
| vous | vous tournez | tournez | turn (formal) |
| nous | nous tournons | tournons | let's turn |

**Examples:**
- **Tourne** à gauche. (Turn left.)
- **Tournez** à droite. (Turn right - formal.)
- **Allez** tout droit. (Go straight ahead.)
- **Marche** tout droit. (Walk straight ahead.)
- **Traverse** la rue. (Cross the street.)
- **Prenez** la première rue à gauche. (Take the first street on the left.)
- **Suivez** cette rue. (Follow this street.)
- **Continuez** tout droit. (Continue straight ahead.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Pronouns**: The imperative form uses the same verb form as the present tense, but without the subject pronoun.
   - Tu tournes → Tourne (You turn → Turn)
   - Vous tournez → Tournez (You turn → Turn - formal)
   - Nous tournons → Tournons (We turn → Let's turn)

2. **Reflexive Verbs**: For reflexive verbs, the reflexive pronoun comes AFTER the verb in the imperative.
   - **Assieds-toi**! (Sit down!)
   - **Asseyez-vous**! (Sit down - formal!)
   - **Levez-vous**! (Stand up - formal!)

3. **Negative Imperative**: For negative commands, use "ne...pas" around the verb.
   - **Ne tourne pas** à gauche. (Don't turn left.)
   - **Ne tournez pas** à droite. (Don't turn right - formal.)
   - **N'allez pas** tout droit. (Don't go straight ahead.)

## Grammar: Describing Locations

### "Être" with Prepositions

Use "être" (to be) with prepositions to describe where things are located:

| French | English | Pronunciation |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| C'est... | It is... | /say/ |
| Il est... | He/It is... | /eel ay/ |
| Elle est... | She/It is... | /ell ay/ |
| Ils sont... | They are... | /eel son/ |
| Elles sont... | They are... (feminine) | /ell son/ |

**Examples:**
- Le musée **est** **dans** le centre-ville. (The museum is in the city center.)
- La gare **est** **à** 5 minutes à pied. (The train station is 5 minutes walk.)
- Le parc **est** **près de** la rivière. (The park is near the river.)
- Le restaurant **est** **en face de** l'hôtel. (The restaurant is opposite the hotel.)
- La pharmacie **est** **au coin de** la rue. (The pharmacy is at the corner of the street.)

### "Il y a" for Existence

Use "il y a" (there is/there are) to indicate the existence of something:

| French | English | Pronunciation |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| Il y a... | There is/are... | /ee lee a/ |
| Il n'y a pas de... | There is/are no... | /eel nee a pa duh/ |

**Examples:**
- **Il y a** un parc près d'ici. (There is a park near here.)
- **Il y a** une pharmacie au coin de la rue. (There is a pharmacy at the corner of the street.)
- **Il n'y a pas de** supermarché ici. (There is no supermarket here.)

## Cultural Note: Getting Around in France

France has an excellent transportation infrastructure, making it easy to get around. Here are some insights into transportation and directions in France:

**Public Transportation**:
- **Métro**: Subway systems are available in major cities like Paris, Lyon, Marseille, Lille, and others. They're efficient and cover most of the city.
- **Bus**: Bus networks complement the metro systems and often reach areas not served by the metro.
- **Tramway**: Tram systems are being developed in many cities and are often integrated with the bus and metro networks.
- **RER (Réseau Express Régional)**: A commuter rail system in the Paris region that connects the city center with the suburbs.
- **TGV (Train à Grande Vitesse)**: High-speed trains that connect major cities in France and other European countries.
- **TER (Transport Express Régional)**: Regional trains that connect smaller towns and cities.

**Tickets and Fares**:
- **Single Tickets**: For one-time use on buses, metro, or trams.
- **Carnet**: A booklet of 10 single tickets, often at a discounted price.
- **Pass**: A pass that allows unlimited travel for a certain period (day, week, month).
- **Navigo**: In Paris, the Navigo pass allows unlimited travel on all public transportation for a week or month.
- **Validation**: Always validate your ticket before boarding (composter votre billet). Failure to do so can result in a fine.

**Maps and Signs**:
- **Plans**: Maps are available at tourist offices, hotels, and often at metro stations.
- **Panneaux**: Signs are posted throughout cities to help with navigation.
- **Flèches**: Arrows on signs indicate directions.
- **Noms de rues**: Street names are posted on signs at intersections.
- **Numéros**: House numbers are posted on buildings, often on a plaque near the door.

**Address Format**:
- French addresses typically include:
  - Number and street name (ex: 15 Rue de Rivoli)
  - Postal code (ex: 75001 for Paris)
  - City name (ex: Paris)
- The format is: Number + Street, Postal Code + City
- Example: 15 Rue de Rivoli, 75001 Paris

**Postal Codes**:
- French postal codes (codes postaux) are 5 digits.
- The first two digits often correspond to the department (administrative division).
- For example:
  - 75xxx: Paris
  - 69xxx: Lyon
  - 13xxx: Marseille
  - 33xxx: Bordeaux

**Street Names**:
- French street names often include:
  - **Rue**: Street (most common)
  - **Avenue**: Avenue
  - **Boulevard**: Boulevard
  - **Place**: Square
  - **Quai**: Quay (along a river)
  - **Impasse**: Dead-end street
  - **Allée**: Lane or alley
  - **Chemin**: Path or road (often in rural areas)
  - **Route**: Road or highway

**Landmarks**:
- Many French cities have distinctive landmarks that can help with navigation:
  - **Paris**: Eiffel Tower, Arc de Triomphe, Notre-Dame Cathedral, Sacré-Cœur Basilica
  - **Lyon**: Basilique Notre-Dame de Fourvière, Vieux Lyon, Parc de la Tête d'Or
  - **Marseille**: Vieux Port, Basilique Notre-Dame de la Garde, Château d'If
  - **Bordeaux**: Place de la Bourse, Cathédrale Saint-André, Cité du Vin
  - **Nice**: Promenade des Anglais, Colline du Château, Vieux Nice

**Asking for Help**:
- The French are generally helpful when asked for directions, especially if you greet them politely first.
- It's always a good idea to start with "Excusez-moi, monsieur/madame" (Excuse me, sir/ma'am) before asking for help.
- If you don't understand the directions, don't hesitate to ask for clarification: "Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît?" (Can you repeat, please?)
- Thank the person with "Merci beaucoup!" (Thank you very much!)

**GPS and Maps**:
- GPS navigation systems work well in France, but it's always a good idea to have a paper map as a backup.
- Google Maps and other mapping apps work well in most areas.
- Some rural areas may have limited GPS coverage.
- Paper maps are available at tourist offices and some hotels.

**Walking**:
- Many French cities are very walkable, with pedestrian-friendly streets and sidewalks.
- Walking is often the best way to explore and discover the charm of French cities.
- Be aware of your surroundings, especially in tourist areas where pickpocketing can occur.
- Always look both ways before crossing the street, as traffic can come from unexpected directions.

**Bicycling**:
- Many French cities have bike-sharing systems, such as Vélib' in Paris.
- Bicycling is a great way to get around and see the city.
- Always wear a helmet and follow traffic rules.
- Be aware that bike lanes may not be available in all areas.

**Driving**:
- Driving in French cities can be challenging due to narrow streets, one-way streets, and limited parking.
- Parking can be expensive and difficult to find in city centers.
- Many cities have restricted zones (zones à accès réglementé) where only authorized vehicles can enter.
- Always have your driver's license, insurance, and registration with you when driving.

**Taxis**:
- Taxis are available in most cities and can be hailed on the street or found at taxi stands.
- Ride-sharing apps like Uber also operate in many French cities.
- Always confirm the fare before starting your journey, or ask the driver to use the meter (compteur).
- Tipping is not required but is appreciated for good service.
