**Context**: Sophie and Marie are shopping at a local market in Lyon.

---

**Marie**: Sophie, on va au marché ce matin? J'ai besoin d'acheter des fruits et des légumes.
**Sophie**: Bonne idée! Moi, j'ai besoin de fromage et de pain.

**Marie**: Parfait! Allons au marché des Halles. Il y a de tout là-bas.
**Sophie**: D'accord. On y va à pied?

**Marie**: Oui, c'est à 10 minutes à pied. C'est très pratique.

## Grammar: Comparative Adjectives

### Forming Comparatives

To compare two things in French, use:

**plus + adjective + que** = more ... than
**moins + adjective + que** = less ... than
**aussi + adjective + que** = as ... as

**Examples:**
- Ce pull est **plus cher que** celui-là. (This sweater is more expensive than that one.)
- Cette robe est **moins chère que** l'autre. (This dress is less expensive than the other one.)
- Ce magasin est **aussi grand que** l'autre. (This store is as big as the other one.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Adjective Agreement**: The adjective agrees with the first noun being compared
   - Ce pull est plus **cher** que celui-là. (masculine)
   - Cette robe est plus **chère** que l'autre. (feminine)

2. **Irregular Comparatives**:
   - bon (good) → meilleur (better)
   - mauvais (bad) → pire (worse)
   - petit (small) → moindre (less - for abstract concepts)

   Examples:
   - Ce restaurant est **meilleur que** l'autre. (This restaurant is better than the other one.)
   - Ce vin est **pire que** celui-là. (This wine is worse than that one.)

## Grammar: Superlative Adjectives

### Forming Superlatives

To express the "most" or "least" of something, use:

**le/la/les plus + adjective** = the most ...
**le/la/les moins + adjective** = the least ...

**Examples:**
- C'est **le magasin le plus cher** de la ville. (It's the most expensive store in the city.)
- C'est **la robe la moins chère** du magasin. (It's the least expensive dress in the store.)
- Ce sont **les fruits les plus frais** du marché. (These are the freshest fruits at the market.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Article Agreement**: The definite article (le/la/les) agrees with the noun
   - le **plus cher** (masculine singular)
   - la **plus chère** (feminine singular)
   - les **plus chers** (masculine plural)
   - les **plus chères** (feminine plural)

2. **Irregular Superlatives**:
   - bon → le meilleur (the best)
   - mauvais → le pire (the worst)
   - petit → le moindre (the least - for abstract concepts)

   Examples:
   - C'est **le meilleur** restaurant de la ville. (It's the best restaurant in the city.)
   - C'est **le pire** vin que j'ai jamais goûté. (It's the worst wine I've ever tasted.)

## Grammar: French Currency

### Euros and Cents

In France, the currency is the euro (€).

**Pronunciation:**
- 1 € = un euro (/on uh-roh/)
- 2 € = deux euros (/duh uh-roh/)
- 10 € = dix euros (/dee uh-roh/)
- 100 € = cent euros (/son uh-roh/)

**Cents:**
- 1 cent = un centime (/on son-teem/)
- 50 cents = cinquante centimes (/sank-on-tuh son-teem/)

### Prices

**Expressing Prices:**
- 5,99 € = cinq euros quatre-vingt-dix-neuf centimes (/sank uh-roh ka-truh-vahn dee-nuhf son-teem/)
- 12,50 € = douze euros cinquante centimes (/dooz uh-roh sank-on-tuh son-teem/)

**Common Phrases:**
- Ça coûte 5 euros. (It costs 5 euros.)
- C'est 10 euros. (It's 10 euros.)
- Ça fait 20 euros. (That makes 20 euros.)
- Le total est de 30 euros. (The total is 30 euros.)

## Cultural Note: Shopping in France

Shopping in France is an important part of daily life and culture. Here are some insights into French shopping habits:

**Types of Stores**:
- **Boulangeries**: French bakeries are found in every neighborhood. They sell fresh bread, pastries, and often sandwiches. The French often buy fresh bread daily.
- **Fromageries**: Cheese shops are common and offer a wide variety of French cheeses. The fromager (cheese seller) can often give advice on which cheeses to buy and how to serve them.
- **Boucheries**: Butcher shops sell a variety of meats. The boucher (butcher) can prepare cuts of meat to your specifications.
- **Poissonneries**: Fish shops are common in coastal areas and cities. They sell fresh fish and seafood.
- **Marchés**: Outdoor markets are held regularly in most towns and cities. They sell fresh produce, cheese, meat, fish, and other goods. Markets are social gathering places and an important part of French culture.
- **Supermarchés**: Supermarkets sell a wide variety of goods, from groceries to household items. They're convenient but often more expensive than specialty shops.
- **Hypermarchés**: Large hypermarkets, often located on the outskirts of cities, sell everything from groceries to electronics to clothing.

**Shopping Hours**:
- Most shops are open from around 9:00 or 10:00 AM to 7:00 or 8:00 PM.
- Many shops close for a long lunch break, typically from 12:00 to 2:00 PM.
- On Sundays, most shops are closed, except for some bakeries, newsstands, and shops in tourist areas.
- Some shops, especially in smaller towns, may close on Monday mornings.

**Market Culture**:
- Markets (marchés) are an important part of French culture. They're social gathering places where people can buy fresh, local products.
- There are different types of markets:
  - **Marché alimentaire**: Food market
  - **Marché aux puces**: Flea market
  - **Marché de Noël**: Christmas market
  - **Brocante**: Antique market
- At markets, it's common to bargain slightly, especially for larger purchases or at flea markets.
- The French often develop relationships with their favorite merchants at the market.

**Bread Culture**:
- Bread (pain) is a staple of French cuisine. The baguette is the most iconic French bread.
- The French often buy fresh bread daily from their local boulangerie.
- There are many types of bread in France, including:
  - **Baguette**: The classic French bread
  - **Pain de campagne**: Country bread
  - **Pain aux céréales**: Whole grain bread
  - **Pain de seigle**: Rye bread
  - **Brioche**: A sweet, buttery bread
  - **Croissant**: A buttery, flaky pastry

**Cheese Culture**:
- France has over 1,000 varieties of cheese, and cheese is an important part of French cuisine.
- The French often buy cheese from their local fromagerie.
- Common types of French cheese include:
  - **Camembert**: A soft, creamy cheese from Normandy
  - **Brie**: A soft, creamy cheese from Île-de-France
  - **Roquefort**: A blue cheese from the south of France
  - **Comté**: A hard cheese from the Jura region
  - **Chèvre**: Goat cheese, which comes in many varieties
  - **Reblochon**: A semi-soft cheese from the Alps

**Payment Methods**:
- Cash (espèces) is still commonly used in France, especially for smaller purchases.
- Credit and debit cards (cartes bancaires) are widely accepted, but some smaller shops may have a minimum purchase amount for card payments.
- Checks (chèques) are still used in France, although less commonly than in the past.
- Contactless payments (paiement sans contact) are becoming increasingly common.
- Mobile payments (paiement mobile) are also growing in popularity.

**Sales and Discounts**:
- **Soldes**: France has two official sales periods per year:
  - **Soldes d'hiver**: Winter sales, typically from early January to early February
  - **Soldes d'été**: Summer sales, typically from late June to early August
- During the soldes, shops can offer significant discounts (up to 70% off).
- **Réductions**: Discounts may be offered at other times of the year, especially for students, seniors, or large families.
- **Promotions**: Special promotions may be offered on specific products or for limited periods.

**Tipping**:
- Tipping is not as expected in France as in some other countries.
- In restaurants, the service charge (service compris) is often included in the bill. If it's not, a tip of 10-15% is appropriate.
- For other services, such as taxis or hotel staff, a small tip (1-2 euros) is appreciated for good service.

**Bag Culture**:
- In France, it's common to bring your own reusable bags (sacs réutilisables) when shopping.
- Many shops sell reusable bags at the checkout.
- Plastic bags (sacs en plastique) are becoming less common due to environmental concerns.
- Some shops may charge a small fee for bags.

**Recycling**:
- France has a strong recycling culture. Many shops have separate containers for different types of waste.
- Common recycling categories include:
  - **Verre**: Glass
  - **Plastique**: Plastic
  - **Papier**: Paper
  - **Métal**: Metal
  - **Déchets organiques**: Organic waste
