# A2 Unit 09: Education and Learning

## Learning Objectives
- Learn vocabulary for education and learning
- Talk about your education and academic background
- Describe school subjects and activities
- Use the verb "apprendre" (to learn)
- Use the verb "enseigner" (to teach)
- Talk about educational goals and achievements
- Understand the French education system

## Dialogue: Talking About Education

**Context**: Sophie and Marie are discussing their educational backgrounds.

---

**Sophie**: Marie, tu as fait quelles études?
**Marie**: J'ai fait des études de droit à l'université. J'ai obtenu ma licence il y a 5 ans.

**Sophie**: C'est impressionnant! Et tu as continué tes études après la licence?
**Marie**: Oui, j'ai fait un master en droit des affaires. J'ai terminé il y a 3 ans.

**Sophie**: Et maintenant, tu travailles dans le domaine du droit?
**Marie**: Oui, je suis avocate dans un cabinet à Lyon. Et toi, qu'est-ce que tu as étudié?

**Sophie**: Moi, j'ai étudié les langues. J'ai une licence en langues étrangères appliquées.
**Marie**: Super! Quelles langues parles-tu?

**Sophie**: Je parle français, anglais, espagnol et un peu d'allemand.
**Marie**: Waouh! Tu es polyglotte! Tu as étudié à l'étranger?

**Sophie**: Oui, j'ai passé un an en Angleterre pendant mes études. C'était une expérience incroyable!
**Marie**: Je suis sûre que ça t'a beaucoup aidée pour ton niveau d'anglais.

**Sophie**: Oui, absolument! Et toi, tu as étudié à l'étranger?
**Marie**: Non, mais j'aimerais bien faire un stage à l'étranger un jour.

## Dialogue: At School

**Context**: A conversation between a teacher and a student.

---

**Professeur**: Bonjour, classe! Aujourd'hui, nous allons parler de l'histoire de France.
**Élève**: Monsieur, est-ce que nous allons avoir un contrôle sur ce chapitre?

**Professeur**: Oui, il y aura un contrôle la semaine prochaine. Vous devez réviser les dates importantes.
**Élève**: Quelles sont les dates importantes que nous devons connaître?

**Professeur**: Vous devez connaître la date de la Révolution française, de la Première Guerre mondiale et de la Seconde Guerre mondiale.
**Élève**: Est-ce que nous devons aussi connaître les noms des présidents?

**Professeur**: Oui, c'est une bonne idée. Vous devez aussi savoir où et quand ces événements se sont passés.
**Élève**: D'accord. Est-ce que nous pouvons faire un exercice en groupe pour réviser?

**Professeur**: Bonne idée! Formez des groupes de 4 et commencez à réviser ensemble.
**Élève**: Merci, monsieur!

## Grammar: The Verb "Apprendre" (To Learn)

### Present Tense of "Apprendre"

| Subject | French | Pronunciation |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| I | j'apprends | /zhap-pron/ |
| You (singular) | tu apprends | /too a-pron/ |
| He/She/It | il/elle apprend | /eel/ell a-pron/ |
| We | nous apprenons | /noo a-pruh-non/ |
| You (plural) | vous apprenez | /voo a-pruh-nay/ |
| They | ils/elles apprennent | /eel/ell a-prenn/ |

**Examples:**
- **J'apprends** le français. (I am learning French.)
- **Tu apprends** l'espagnol. (You are learning Spanish.)
- **Il apprend** les mathématiques. (He is learning mathematics.)
- **Nous apprenons** l'histoire. (We are learning history.)
- **Vous apprenez** la biologie. (You are learning biology.)
- **Ils apprennent** la physique. (They are learning physics.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Direct Object**: "Apprendre" can be used with a **direct object** (what you're learning).
   - J'apprends **le français**. (I am learning French.)
   - Tu apprends **les mathématiques**. (You are learning mathematics.)

2. **Infinitive**: "Apprendre" can also be used with an **infinitive** verb.
   - J'apprends **à nager**. (I am learning to swim.)
   - Tu apprends **à jouer de la guitare**. (You are learning to play the guitar.)

3. **Negative Form**: "ne...pas" surrounds the verb.
   - Je **n'apprends pas** l'allemand. (I am not learning German.)
   - Il **n'apprend pas** les mathématiques. (He is not learning mathematics.)

4. **Question Form**: Invert subject and verb.
   - **Apprends**-tu l'espagnol? (Are you learning Spanish?)
   - **Apprend**-il le français? (Is he learning French?)

## Grammar: The Verb "Enseigner" (To Teach)

### Present Tense of "Enseigner"

| Subject | French | Pronunciation |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| I | j'enseigne | /zhan-senh/ |
| You (singular) | tu enseignes | /too an-senh/ |
| He/She/It | il/elle enseigne | /eel/ell an-senh/ |
| We | nous enseignons | /noo an-senh-on/ |
| You (plural) | vous enseignez | /voo an-senh-ay/ |
| They | ils/elles enseignent | /eel/ell an-senh/ |

**Examples:**
- **J'enseigne** le français. (I teach French.)
- **Tu enseignes** les mathématiques. (You teach mathematics.)
- **Il enseigne** l'histoire. (He teaches history.)
- **Nous enseignons** la biologie. (We teach biology.)
- **Vous enseignez** la physique. (You teach physics.)
- **Ils enseignent** la chimie. (They teach chemistry.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Direct Object**: "Enseigner" is used with a **direct object** (what you're teaching).
   - J'enseigne **le français**. (I teach French.)
   - Tu enseignes **les mathématiques**. (You teach mathematics.)

2. **Indirect Object**: "Enseigner" can also be used with an **indirect object** (to whom you're teaching).
   - J'enseigne **aux enfants**. (I teach children.)
   - Il enseigne **à l'université**. (He teaches at the university.)

3. **Both Objects**: "Enseigner" can be used with both a direct and indirect object.
   - J'enseigne **le français aux enfants**. (I teach French to children.)
   - Il enseigne **les mathématiques aux étudiants**. (He teaches mathematics to students.)

4. **Negative Form**: "ne...pas" surrounds the verb.
   - Je **n'enseigne pas** l'allemand. (I don't teach German.)
   - Il **n'enseigne pas** les mathématiques. (He doesn't teach mathematics.)

## Cultural Note: Education in France

The French education system is known for its **rigor, structure, and emphasis on academic excellence**. Here are some key aspects of education in France:

**Structure of the Education System**:
- **École Maternelle** (ages 3-6): Preschool/kindergarten, focusing on **socialization, basic skills, and play-based learning**.
- **École Primaire** (ages 6-11): Primary school, divided into:
  - **CP, CE1, CE2** (ages 6-8): Learning to read, write, and basic math.
  - **CM1, CM2** (ages 9-11): More advanced skills in reading, writing, math, and introduction to other subjects.
- **Collège** (ages 11-15): Middle school, with a **common core curriculum** (tronc commun) and **electives** (options). Students take the **Brevet des Collèges** exam at the end of 3ème.
- **Lycée** (ages 15-18): High school, with three main tracks:
  - **Lycée Général**: Academic track, preparing for the **Baccalauréat Général** and university studies.
  - **Lycée Technologique**: Technical track, preparing for the **Baccalauréat Technologique** and further technical studies.
  - **Lycée Professionnel**: Vocational track, preparing for the **CAP (Certificat d'Aptitude Professionnelle)** or **BEP (Brevet d'Études Professionnelles)** and entry into the workforce.
- **Higher Education**: Includes **universities** (universités), **grandes écoles** (elite higher education institutions), and **vocational schools** (écoles spécialisées).

**The Baccalauréat (Bac)**:
- The **baccalauréat** (often called the **bac**) is the **high school diploma** in France, taken at the end of **lycée** (around age 18).
- It is a **national exam** that determines **university eligibility** and **future opportunities**.
- There are **three main types** of bac:
  - **Bac Général**: For students in the **general academic track**, with specializations in **literature, economics, or sciences**.
  - **Bac Technologique**: For students in the **technological track**, with specializations in various technical fields.
  - **Bac Professionnel**: For students in the **vocational track**, focusing on **specific trades or professions**.
- The bac is **highly competitive** and requires **extensive preparation** throughout lycée.
- **Passing the bac** is a **major milestone** in French education and is celebrated with **parties and family gatherings**.

**Grades and Evaluation**:
- The French grading system uses a **scale from 0 to 20**, with:
  - **20/20**: Perfect (very rare)
  - **16-19/20**: Very good (très bien)
  - **14-15/20**: Good (bien)
  - **12-13/20**: Satisfactory (assez bien)
  - **10-11/20**: Pass (passable)
  - **Below 10/20**: Fail (insuffisant)
- **La moyenne** (the average) is typically **10/20**, and students must maintain an average of at least **10/20** to pass.
- **Redoubler** (to repeat a year) is **common** if a student fails to meet the required standards.
- **Mentions** (honors) are awarded on the bac:
  - **Mention Très Bien** (16/20 and above)
  - **Mention Bien** (14/20 and above)
  - **Mention Assez Bien** (12/20 and above)
  - **Mention Passable** (10/20 and above)

**School Day**:
- The **school day** in France is typically **longer** than in many other countries, with classes often running from **8:00 AM to 5:00 or 6:00 PM**.
- **Lunch break** (pause déjeuner) is usually **1-2 hours**, and many students go home for lunch.
- **Wednesday afternoons** are often **free** for primary school students, with many participating in **extracurricular activities** (activités extrascolaires).
- **Saturdays** may have **classes** for some students, especially in **collège and lycée**.
- **School year** (année scolaire) typically runs from **early September to early July**, with **holidays** (vacances scolaires) throughout the year.

**School Holidays**:
- France has a **three-zone system** for school holidays, with different regions having **slightly different schedules**.
- The main school holidays include:
  - **Toussaint** (All Saints' Day): 2 weeks in **late October/early November**.
  - **Noël** (Christmas): 2 weeks in **late December/early January**.
  - **Hiver** (Winter): 2 weeks in **February/March**.
  - **Printemps** (Spring): 2 weeks in **April**.
  - **Été** (Summer): 8 weeks in **July/August**.
- **Zone A, B, and C**: France is divided into **three zones** for school holidays, with each zone having **slightly different dates** to **stagger vacations** and **reduce traffic**.

**School Subjects**:
- The French school curriculum includes a **wide range of subjects**, with an emphasis on **academic rigor** and **critical thinking**.
- **Core subjects** (matières principales) include:
  - **Français** (French language and literature)
  - **Mathématiques** (Mathematics)
  - **Histoire-Géographie** (History-Geography)
  - **Sciences** (Sciences: Physics, Chemistry, Biology)
  - **Langues Vivantes** (Modern Languages: English, Spanish, German, etc.)
- **Secondary subjects** (matières secondaires) include:
  - **Éducation Physique et Sportive (EPS)** (Physical Education)
  - **Arts Plastiques** (Art)
  - **Musique** (Music)
  - **Technologie** (Technology)
  - **Éducation Civique** (Civics)
- **Electives** (options) are offered in **collège and lycée**, allowing students to **specialize** in areas of interest.

**Extracurricular Activities**:
- **Activités extrascolaires** (extracurricular activities) are **popular** in France and include:
  - **Sports**: Football, rugby, judo, swimming, etc.
  - **Music**: Piano, violin, guitar, etc.
  - **Art**: Drawing, painting, sculpture, etc.
  - **Dance**: Ballet, modern dance, hip-hop, etc.
  - **Theater**: Acting, improvisation, etc.
  - **Scouts**: Scouting organizations (Éclaireurs et Éclaireuses de France, Scouts et Guides de France, etc.)
- Extracurricular activities are often offered through **schools, clubs, or private organizations**.
- **Conservatoires** (music and arts schools) offer **high-quality training** in music, dance, and theater.

**Higher Education**:
- **Universities** (universités) are **public institutions** that offer a **wide range of programs** at the **bachelor's (licence), master's (master), and PhD (doctorat) levels**.
- **Grandes Écoles** are **elite higher education institutions** that are **highly selective** and **prestigious**. They include:
  - **Écoles d'Ingénieurs** (Engineering Schools): Polytechnique, Centrale, Mines, etc.
  - **Écoles de Commerce** (Business Schools): HEC, ESSEC, ESCP, etc.
  - **Écoles Normales Supérieures (ENS)** (Elite Teacher Training Schools)
  - **Sciences Po** (Institute of Political Studies)
- **Admission** to grandes écoles is typically through **competitive exams** (concours) after **2 years of preparatory classes** (classes préparatoires or "prépa").
- **Vocational Schools** (écoles spécialisées) offer **practical training** in specific fields, such as **art, design, hospitality, etc.**.

**Grading and Exams**:
- **Exams** (examens) are a **central part** of the French education system, with **frequent testing** throughout the school year.
- **Partial exams** (partiels) are **midterm exams** taken at the end of each **semester** (semestre).
- **Final exams** (examens finaux) are taken at the end of the **school year** (année scolaire) or **degree program** (formation).
- **Oral exams** (oraux) are **common** in France and require students to **explain their answers verbally**.
- **Written exams** (écrits) often include **essays** (dissertations) and **problem-solving questions** (problèmes).
- **Cheating** (tricher) is **strictly prohibited** and can result in **severe penalties**, including **expulsion** (exclusion).

**Homework and Study**:
- **Homework** (devoirs) is a **significant part** of the French education system, with students often spending **several hours per day** on assignments.
- **Devoirs à la maison** (homework) includes **exercises** (exercices), **essays** (rédactions), and **projects** (projets).
- **Study groups** (groupes d'étude) are **common**, with students often working together to **prepare for exams** (réviser pour les examens).
- **Private tutoring** (cours particuliers) is **popular**, with many students receiving **additional help** in specific subjects.
- **Libraries** (bibliothèques) are **widely used** for studying, with many schools having their own **library** (bibliothèque) or **study hall** (salle d'étude).

**School Uniforms**:
- **School uniforms** (uniformes scolaires) are **not common** in France, with most public schools having **no dress code**.
- However, some **private schools** (écoles privées) and **religious schools** (écoles religieuses) may require **uniforms** or have **dress codes**.
- **Casual dress** is common in French schools, with students wearing **comfortable, everyday clothing**.

**School Meals**:
- **School lunches** (cantines scolaires) are **provided** in most schools, with **subsidized meals** (repas subventionnés) available for students.
- **Cantine** (school cafeteria) meals typically include a **starter** (entrée), **main course** (plat principal), **cheese or dessert** (fromage ou dessert), and **bread** (pain).
- **Special dietary needs** (régimes spéciaux) are often **accommodated**, with options for **vegetarian, halal, kosher, or allergy-friendly meals**.
- **Packed lunches** (pique-niques) are also **common**, with many students bringing **homemade meals** from home.

**School Transportation**:
- **School buses** (cars scolaires) are **provided** for students who live **far from school**.
- **Public transportation** (transports en commun) is often used by students, with **discounted fares** (tarifs réduits) available for **students under 18**.
- **Walking or biking** (marche ou vélo) is common for students who live **close to school**.
- **Carpooling** (covoiturage) is also **popular**, with parents often **taking turns** driving groups of students to school.

**Special Education**:
- **Special education** (éducation spécialisée) is **integrated** into the French school system, with **support** (soutien) provided for students with **special needs** (besoins éducatifs particuliers).
- **ULIS** (Unités Localisées pour l'Inclusion Scolaire) are **specialized units** within mainstream schools that provide **support** for students with **disabilities** (handicaps) or **learning difficulties** (difficultés d'apprentissage).
- **SEGPA** (Sections d'Enseignement Général et Professionnel Adapté) are **specialized classes** for students with **significant learning difficulties** (difficultés scolaires importantes).
- **IME** (Instituts Médico-Éducatifs) are **specialized schools** for students with **severe disabilities** (handicaps graves).

**Bilingual and International Education**:
- **Bilingual education** (enseignement bilingue) is **increasingly popular** in France, with many schools offering **bilingual programs** (sections bilingues) in **English, German, Spanish, or other languages**.
- **International schools** (écoles internationales) are available in **major cities**, offering **curricula** in **English or other languages**.
- **European schools** (écoles européennes) offer **multilingual education** and follow a **European curriculum**.
- **Exchange programs** (programmes d'échange) are **common**, with many French students spending **time abroad** and **foreign students** coming to France.

**Educational Reforms**:
- The French education system has **underwent several reforms** in recent years, including:
  - **Réforme du bac** (Bac reform): Introduced in 2019, this reform **reduced the number of exams** and **increased continuous assessment** (contrôle continu).
  - **Réforme du collège** (Middle school reform): Introduced in 2016, this reform **reorganized the curriculum** and **increased flexibility** in subject choices.
  - **Réforme de l'université** (University reform): Aimed at **improving student success rates** and **reducing dropout rates** (taux d'échec).
- These reforms have been **controversial**, with **debates** about their **effectiveness** and **impact on students**.

**Educational Values**:
- The French education system emphasizes **academic excellence** (excellence académique), **critical thinking** (esprit critique), and **cultural knowledge** (culture générale).
- **Secularism** (laïcité) is a **fundamental principle** of the French education system, with **religion** not taught in **public schools**.
- **Equality** (égalité) is a **core value**, with efforts made to provide **equal opportunities** (égalité des chances) for all students.
- **Citizenship** (citoyenneté) is emphasized, with students learning about **French history, culture, and values** (valeurs françaises).
- **Creativity** (créativité) and **innovation** (innovation) are **encouraged**, especially in **art, music, and technology**.

**Challenges in the French Education System**:
- **Inequality** (inégalités): There are **disparities** in educational outcomes between **different socioeconomic groups** (groupes socio-économiques) and **regions** (régions).
- **School dropout** (décrochage scolaire): France has a **higher-than-average dropout rate** compared to other **OECD countries**, with around **10% of students** leaving school without a **diploma**.
- **Teacher shortages** (pénurie d'enseignants): There are **shortages** of teachers in **certain subjects** (mathématiques, sciences, langues) and **regions** (zones rurales, banlieues).
- **School violence** (violence scolaire): **Bullying** (harcèlement) and **violence** in schools are **ongoing concerns**, with efforts being made to **address these issues** (lutter contre ces problèmes).
- **Standardized testing** (évaluations standardisées): There is **debate** about the **role of standardized testing** in the French education system, with some arguing that it **creates stress** (stresse les élèves) and **limits creativity** (limite la créativité).

**Tips for Studying in France**:
- **Learn French**: While some programs are offered in **English**, **learning French** is essential for **full integration** into the French education system and **daily life**.
- **Understand the system**: Familiarize yourself with the **structure** of the French education system, including **degrees, exams, and grading**.
- **Be prepared for rigor**: The French education system is **academically rigorous**, with **high expectations** for students.
- **Participate in class**: **Active participation** (participation active) in class is **encouraged** and can **improve your grades**.
- **Form study groups**: **Working with classmates** (travailler avec des camarades) can help you **understand difficult concepts** and **prepare for exams**.
- **Take advantage of resources**: Use **libraries, tutoring, and online resources** to **supplement your learning**.
- **Manage your time**: **Time management** (gestion du temps) is **key** to **succeeding** in the French education system, with its **heavy workload** (charge de travail importante).
- **Ask for help**: Don't hesitate to **ask teachers or classmates for help** (demander de l'aide aux professeurs ou aux camarades) if you're **struggling** (vous avez des difficultés).
