## Learning Objectives
- Discuss social issues and community concerns
- Express opinions and arguments about social problems

- Use relative pronouns to connect ideas

## Dialogue: Discussing Local Social Issues

**Context**: Sophie and her neighbor Marie are discussing issues in their neighborhood.

---

**Sophie**: Marie, tu as vu les dernières actualités sur la rénovation du quartier?
**Marie**: Oui, et ça me préoccupe. Il paraîtrait qu'ils veulent construire un grand centre commercial.

**Sophie**: Mais nous avons déjà assez de magasins. Ce dont nous avons besoin, c'est plus d'espaces verts.
**Marie**: Je suis tout à fait d'accord. Il faut que la mairie écoute les habitants.

**Sophie**: Justement, il y a une réunion publique la semaine prochaine. Tu veux y aller avec moi?
**Marie**: Bien sûr! Il est important que nous fassions entendre nos voix.

**Sophie**: Et il ne faut pas oublier le problème des sans-abri dans notre quartier.
**Marie**: C'est vrai. J'ai lu qu'une association locale organise une soupe populaire. On pourrait y participer comme bénévoles.

**Sophie**: Bonne idée! Et pour les personnes âgées? Beaucoup sont isolées.
**Marie**: Oui, c'est un vrai problème. Peut-être que nous pourrions organiser des visites régulières.

**Sophie**: Tu as raison. Il faut que chacun fasse sa part pour améliorer notre communauté.

## Dialogue: Debating Social Justice

**Context**: Thomas and his friend Paul are having a discussion about social justice.

---

**Thomas**: Paul, qu'est-ce que tu penses des inégalités sociales dans notre pays?
**Paul**: C'est un sujet complexe. D'un côté, il y a des gens qui travaillent très dur mais qui ont du mal à joindre les deux bouts.

**Thomas**: Exactement. Et de l'autre côté, il y a ceux qui ont tout et qui ne paient pas assez d'impôts.
**Paul**: Je pense qu'il faut que le gouvernement fasse plus pour aider les plus nécessiteux.

**Thomas**: Mais comment? Certains disent qu'il faut augmenter les impôts des riches.
**Paul**: Peut-être, mais il faut aussi que les entreprises paient leur juste part.

**Thomas**: Et qu'est-ce que tu penses de l'éducation? C'est un facteur clé pour réduire les inégalités.
**Paul**: Absolument. Il faut que tout le monde ait accès à une éducation de qualité, quel que soit son milieu social.

**Thomas**: Je suis d'accord. Et il faut aussi lutter contre les discriminations.
**Paul**: Oui, c'est essentiel. Personne ne devrait être traité différemment à cause de sa race, de son genre ou de sa religion.

**Thomas**: C'est vrai. Nous avons tous le droit d'être traités avec dignité et respect.

## Grammar: Subjunctive Mood

### Introduction to Subjunctive

The **subjunctive mood** (le subjonctif) is used to express **doubt, desire, necessity, or emotion** about an action. It's commonly used after certain **trigger expressions** that indicate these attitudes.

**When to use the subjunctive:**
1. **After expressions of doubt or denial**
2. **After expressions of desire or will**
3. **After expressions of necessity or obligation**
4. **After expressions of emotion or feeling**
5. **After certain conjunctions**

### Subjunctive Conjugation

To form the subjunctive, take the **3rd person plural (ils/elles) form of the present tense**, remove **-ent**, and add the **subjunctive endings**.

**Subjunctive Endings:**
| Subject | Ending | Pronunciation |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| je | -e | /uh/ |
| tu | -es | /uh/ |
| il/elle/on | -e | /uh/ |
| nous | -ions | /yon/ |
| vous | -iez | /yeh/ |
| ils/elles | -ent | /on/ |

**Regular Verbs:**
- **Parler** (to speak): que je parl**e**, que tu parl**es**, qu'il parl**e**, que nous parl**ions**, que vous parl**iez**, qu'ils parl**ent**
- **Finir** (to finish): que je finiss**e**, que tu finiss**es**, qu'il finiss**e**, que nous finiss**ions**, que vous finiss**iez**, qu'ils finiss**ent**
- **Vendre** (to sell): que je vend**e**, que tu vend**es**, qu'il vend**e**, que nous vend**ions**, que vous vend**iez**, qu'ils vend**ent**

**Examples:**
- Il faut que je **parle** (I must speak)
- Il faut que tu **finisses** (You must finish)
- Il faut qu'il **vende** (He must sell)
- Il faut que nous **partions** (We must leave)

### Irregular Subjunctive Verbs

Some verbs have **irregular stems** in the subjunctive:

| Infinitive | Subjunctive Stem | Example (que je) |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|
| avoir | aie | que j'**aie** |
| être | soie | que je **sois** |
| aller | aille | que j'**aille** |
| faire | fasse | que je **fasse** |
| pouvoir | puisse | que je **puisse** |
| vouloir | veuille | que je **veuille** |
| savoir | sache | que je **sache** |
| prendre | prenne | que je **prenne** |
| venir | vienne | que je **vienne** |
| tenir | tienne | que je **tienne** |
| voir | voie | que je **voie** |
| envoyer | envoie | que j'**envoie** |

**Examples:**
- Il faut que j'**aie** de l'argent. (I must have money.)
- Il faut que je **sois** à l'heure. (I must be on time.)
- Il faut que j'**aille** au marché. (I must go to the market.)
- Il faut que je **fasse** mes devoirs. (I must do my homework.)
- Il faut que je **puisse** partir. (I must be able to leave.)

### Trigger Expressions for Subjunctive

**Expressions of Doubt or Denial:**
- **Je doute que** (I doubt that) + subjunctive
- **Il est douteux que** (It's doubtful that) + subjunctive
- **Il n'est pas sûr que** (It's not sure that) + subjunctive
- **Il n'est pas certain que** (It's not certain that) + subjunctive
- **Je ne crois pas que** (I don't believe that) + subjunctive
- **Je ne pense pas que** (I don't think that) + subjunctive

**Examples:**
- Je doute qu'il **vienne**. (I doubt that he will come.)
- Il n'est pas sûr que nous **ayons** raison. (It's not sure that we are right.)
- Je ne crois pas qu'elle **sache** la réponse. (I don't believe that she knows the answer.)

**Expressions of Desire or Will:**
- **Je veux que** (I want that) + subjunctive
- **J'aimerais que** (I would like that) + subjunctive
- **Il faut que** (It's necessary that) + subjunctive
- **Il est nécessaire que** (It's necessary that) + subjunctive
- **Il est important que** (It's important that) + subjunctive
- **Il est essentiel que** (It's essential that) + subjunctive
- **Je préfère que** (I prefer that) + subjunctive
- **J'exige que** (I demand that) + subjunctive

**Examples:**
- Je veux que tu **partes**. (I want you to leave.)
- Il faut que nous **aidions**. (We must help.)
- Il est important que tout le monde **participe**. (It's important that everyone participates.)
- Je préfère que vous **restiez**. (I prefer that you stay.)

**Expressions of Necessity or Obligation:**
- **Il faut que** (It's necessary that) + subjunctive
- **Il est nécessaire que** (It's necessary that) + subjunctive
- **Il est obligatoire que** (It's obligatory that) + subjunctive
- **Il est indispensable que** (It's indispensable that) + subjunctive
- **Il est urgent que** (It's urgent that) + subjunctive

**Examples:**
- Il faut que nous **fassions** quelque chose. (We must do something.)
- Il est nécessaire que tu **comprennes**. (It's necessary that you understand.)
- Il est urgent que le gouvernement **agisse**. (It's urgent that the government acts.)

**Expressions of Emotion or Feeling:**
- **Je suis content que** (I'm happy that) + subjunctive
- **Je suis triste que** (I'm sad that) + subjunctive
- **Je suis désolé que** (I'm sorry that) + subjunctive
- **J'ai peur que** (I'm afraid that) + subjunctive
- **Ça me surprend que** (It surprises me that) + subjunctive
- **Ça me dérange que** (It bothers me that) + subjunctive

**Examples:**
- Je suis content que tu **sois** là. (I'm happy that you are here.)
- Je suis désolé qu'il **ait** des problèmes. (I'm sorry that he has problems.)
- J'ai peur que nous **n'y arrivions** pas. (I'm afraid that we won't succeed.)

**Conjunctions that Trigger Subjunctive:**
- **afin que** (so that) + subjunctive
- **pour que** (so that) + subjunctive
- **à moins que** (unless) + subjunctive
- **avant que** (before) + subjunctive
- **bien que** (although) + subjunctive
- **quoi que** (whatever) + subjunctive
- **qui que** (whoever) + subjunctive
- **où que** (wherever) + subjunctive

**Examples:**
- Je fais ça **afin que** tu **comprennes**. (I do this so that you understand.)
- Il faut partir **avant qu'**il ne **fasse** nuit. (We must leave before it gets dark.)
- **Bien que** je **sois** fatigué, je continue. (Although I'm tired, I continue.)

### Subjunctive with Impersonal Expressions

Many **impersonal expressions** (expressions without a specific subject) trigger the subjunctive:

- **Il faut que** (It's necessary that)
- **Il est nécessaire que** (It's necessary that)
- **Il est important que** (It's important that)
- **Il est essentiel que** (It's essential that)
- **Il est urgent que** (It's urgent that)
- **Il est possible que** (It's possible that)
- **Il est douteux que** (It's doubtful that)
- **Il est peu probable que** (It's unlikely that)
- **Il se peut que** (It may be that)

**Examples:**
- **Il faut que** tout le monde **participe**. (Everyone must participate.)
- **Il est important que** nous **agissions** maintenant. (It's important that we act now.)
- **Il est possible que** je **vienne** demain. (It's possible that I'll come tomorrow.)

### Subjunctive vs. Indicative

| Subjunctive | Indicative |
|-------------|------------|
| **Je doute qu'**il **vienne**. (I doubt that he will come.) | **Je pense qu'**il **viendra**. (I think that he will come.) |
| **Il faut que** tu **partes**. (You must leave.) | **Je sais que** tu **parts**. (I know that you are leaving.) |
| **J'ai peur qu'**il **ait** un accident. (I'm afraid that he will have an accident.) | **J'espère qu'**il **aura** un bon voyage. (I hope that he will have a good trip.) |

**Rule of Thumb:**
- Use **subjunctive** when the main clause expresses **doubt, desire, necessity, or emotion**
- Use **indicative** when the main clause expresses **certainty, belief, or fact**

### Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns **connect clauses** and refer to a noun mentioned earlier. They introduce **relative clauses** that give more information about that noun.

**Simple Relative Pronouns:**

| Pronoun | Function | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| qui | subject (who, which, that) | L'homme **qui** parle (The man **who** is speaking) |
| que | direct object (whom, which, that) | L'homme **que** je vois (The man **whom** I see) |
| où | place (where) | La ville **où** j'habite (The city **where** I live) |
| dont | possession (whose, of which) | La femme **dont** le fils est médecin (The woman **whose** son is a doctor) |

**Examples:**
- C'est un problème **qui** touche tout le monde. (It's a problem **that** affects everyone.)
- Voici le livre **que** j'ai lu. (Here is the book **that** I read.)
- C'est l'école **où** j'ai étudié. (It's the school **where** I studied.)
- Voici l'homme **dont** je t'ai parlé. (Here is the man **about whom** I told you.)

**Complex Relative Pronouns:**

| Pronoun | Function | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| lequel | which (masculine singular) | Le problème **lequel** m'intéresse (The problem **which** interests me) |
| laquelle | which (feminine singular) | La solution **laquelle** j'ai choisie (The solution **which** I chose) |
| lesquels | which (masculine plural) | Les problèmes **lesquels** nous avons discutés (The problems **which** we discussed) |
| lesquelles | which (feminine plural) | Les solutions **lesquelles** j'ai proposées (The solutions **which** I proposed) |
| duquel | of which (masculine singular) | L'homme **duquel** je t'ai parlé (The man **of whom** I told you) |
| de laquelle | of which (feminine singular) | La femme **de laquelle** je t'ai parlé (The woman **of whom** I told you) |
| desquels | of which (masculine plural) | Les hommes **desquels** je t'ai parlé (The men **of whom** I told you) |
| desquelles | of which (feminine plural) | Les femmes **desquelles** je t'ai parlé (The women **of whom** I told you) |

**When to use lequel vs. qui/que:**
- Use **lequel/laquelle/etc.** when the relative pronoun is **preceded by a preposition** (à, de, avec, pour, etc.)
- Use **qui/que** when there is **no preposition**

**Examples:**
- C'est un sujet **sur lequel** nous devons réfléchir. (It's a subject **about which** we must think.)
- Voici les gens **avec qui** j'ai travaillé. (Here are the people **with whom** I worked.)
- C'est une association **à laquelle** je participe. (It's an association **to which** I participate.)

## Cultural Note: Social Issues and Community in France

France has a **rich tradition of social activism** and **community involvement**. The French take **social issues seriously** and have a **strong sense of solidarity**. Here's an overview of social issues and community life in France:

### French Social Model:

France is known for its **strong social protection system**, often referred to as the **"French social model"** (modèle social français). This system includes:

- **Universal Healthcare**: France has a **universal healthcare system** (sécurité sociale) that provides **high-quality medical care** to all residents, regardless of income.
- **Unemployment Benefits**: Workers who lose their jobs receive **unemployment benefits** (allocations chômage) to help them through difficult periods.
- **Family Allowances**: Families receive **monthly allowances** (allocations familiales) to help with the cost of raising children.
- **Retirement Pensions**: Retired workers receive **pensions** based on their contributions during their working years.
- **Minimum Income**: The **Revenu de Solidarité Active (RSA)** provides a **minimum income** for those who cannot work.
- **Housing Benefits**: Low-income individuals and families can receive **housing benefits** (aides au logement) to help with rent.

**Funding**: This system is funded through **social security contributions** (cotisations sociales) paid by both **employers and employees**.

### Social Issues in France:

**Poverty and Inequality:**
- **Poverty Rate**: About **14% of the population** lives below the poverty line in France.
- **Income Inequality**: France has **less income inequality** than the US but **more than** some Nordic countries.
- **Wealth Gap**: The **wealthiest 10%** own about **50% of the total wealth**, while the **poorest 50%** own about **10%**.
- **Child Poverty**: About **20% of children** live in poverty, with **higher rates** in single-parent families.
- **Working Poor**: Some people have jobs but still **live in poverty** due to **low wages and high cost of living**.

**Housing Issues:**
- **Housing Crisis**: There is a **housing crisis** in many French cities, particularly **Paris**, where **rents are very high** and **affordable housing is scarce**.
- **Homelessness**: There are **over 200,000 homeless people** in France, with **Paris having the highest number**.
- **Slums**: There are **informal settlements** (bidonvilles) in some areas, particularly around **Paris and Marseille**.
- **Gentrification**: Many **working-class neighborhoods** in Paris and other cities are **undergoing gentrification**, leading to **displacement of long-time residents**.

**Unemployment:**
- **Unemployment Rate**: The **unemployment rate** in France is around **7-8%**, higher than the **EU average**.
- **Youth Unemployment**: **Youth unemployment** is particularly high, at around **20%**.
- **Long-Term Unemployment**: About **40% of unemployed people** have been **without work for over a year**.
- **Regional Disparities**: Unemployment rates **vary significantly by region**, with **higher rates in the north and in overseas territories**.

**Discrimination and Inequality:**
- **Racial Discrimination**: People from **North African and Sub-Saharan African backgrounds** face **significant discrimination** in **housing, employment, and policing**.
- **Gender Inequality**: While France has made **progress in gender equality**, there are still **gaps in pay, political representation, and domestic responsibilities**.
- **LGBTQ+ Rights**: France has **strong legal protections** for LGBTQ+ individuals, including **same-sex marriage** (since 2013) and **adoption rights**.
- **Disability Rights**: France has **laws protecting the rights of people with disabilities**, but **accessibility remains a challenge** in many areas.

**Immigration and Integration:**
- **Immigration**: About **12% of France's population** is **foreign-born**, with the **largest groups** coming from **Algeria, Morocco, Portugal, and Italy**.
- **Integration**: France has a **republican model of integration** that emphasizes **assimilation into French culture**.
- **Undocumented Immigrants**: There are **estimated 300,000-400,000 undocumented immigrants** in France.
- **Asylum Seekers**: France receives **tens of thousands of asylum applications** each year, with **acceptance rates around 25%**.
- **Islamophobia**: There has been **rising Islamophobia** in France, particularly after **terrorist attacks**.

**Education and Social Mobility:**
- **Education System**: France's education system is **highly centralized** and **largely public**, with **free education** from **primary school to university**.
- **School Dropout**: The **school dropout rate** is around **10%**, with **higher rates in disadvantaged areas**.
- **Social Mobility**: France has **less social mobility** than some other European countries, with **family background** playing a **significant role in educational and professional success**.
- **Elite Schools**: The **grandes écoles** system **reproduces social inequalities**, with **students from privileged backgrounds** being **overrepresented**.

### Community Life in France:

**Associations and NGOs:**
- France has a **vibrant civil society** with **over 1.3 million associations** (non-profit organizations).
- **Associations Law**: The **1901 Associations Law** allows **groups of people to form associations** without **government approval**.
- **Popular Causes**: The **most popular causes** for associations include **sports, culture, social services, and environmental protection**.
- **Volunteering**: About **23% of French people** are **involved in volunteering** in some capacity.

**Social Movements:**
- **Labor Movement**: France has a **strong labor movement** with **high union membership** (about **11% of workers**).
- **Student Movements**: Student protests have **played a significant role** in French politics, most notably in **May 1968**.
- **Environmental Movement**: The environmental movement has **grown significantly** in recent years, with **climate change** being a **major concern**.
- **Feminist Movement**: The feminist movement has **gained momentum**, particularly with the **#MeToo movement**.
- **Anti-Racism Movement**: There are **numerous anti-racism organizations** working to **combat discrimination**.

**Social Protests:**
- **Strikes**: **Strikes** (grèves) are **common** in France and are **protected by law**.
- **Demonstrations**: **Demonstrations** (manifestations) are a **regular part of French political life**.
- **Yellow Vests**: The **Yellow Vests** (Gilets Jaunes) movement was a **major protest movement** that began in **2018** over **economic inequality and fuel taxes**.
- **Pension Reforms**: There have been **massive protests** against **pension reforms**, most recently in **2023**.
- **Police Violence**: There have been **concerns about police violence** during protests, particularly the **use of LBDs** (flash-ball guns).

### French Social Values:

- **Solidarity**: **Solidarity** (solidarité) is a **core French value**, with **strong support for social protection systems**.
- **Equality**: **Equality** (égalité) is a **fundamental principle** of the French Republic, as stated in the **motto "Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité"**.
- **Secularism**: **Secularism** (laïcité) is a **key principle** in France, with **religion being separate from the state**.
- **Republican Values**: The French Republic is based on **republican values** of **liberty, equality, and fraternity**.
- **Social Justice**: There is a **strong belief** in **social justice** and the **role of the state in ensuring fairness**.

### French Social Programs and Initiatives:

- **Restos du Cœur**: A **network of food banks** that provides **free meals** to those in need.
- **Secours Populaire**: A **non-profit organization** that provides **various forms of assistance** to those in need.
- **Secours Catholique**: A **Catholic charity** that provides **social services** to the poor.
- **Emmaüs**: A **solidarity movement** that **fights poverty** through **various initiatives**, including **recycling and social enterprises**.
- **ATD Quart Monde**: An **anti-poverty organization** that works to **eliminate extreme poverty**.
- **La Croix-Rouge Française**: The **French Red Cross**, which provides **emergency assistance, healthcare, and social services**.

**Government Programs:**
- **Revenu de Solidarité Active (RSA)**: A **minimum income program** for those who cannot work.
- **Prime d'Activité**: A **supplement to low incomes** for working people.
- **Aide Médicale de l'État (AME)**: **Medical assistance** for **undocumented immigrants**.
- **Aide au Logement**: **Housing benefits** for low-income individuals and families.
- **Allocation de Rentrée Scolaire (ARS)**: **Back-to-school allowance** to help families with school expenses.

### Social Housing in France:

- **HLM**: **Habitations à Loyer Modéré** (HLM) are **social housing units** with **subsidized rents**.
- **Waiting Lists**: There are **long waiting lists** for social housing, particularly in **large cities**.
- **Social Mixing**: French social housing policy aims for **social mixing** (mixité sociale) in neighborhoods.
- **Renovation**: There are **programs to renovate** social housing and **improve living conditions**.

### French Social Innovations:

- **Social Economy**: France has a **strong social economy** sector, with **cooperatives, associations, and social enterprises**.
- **Social Entrepreneurship**: There is a **growing social entrepreneurship** movement in France.
- **Participatory Budgeting**: Some cities have **implemented participatory budgeting**, allowing **residents to decide how to spend part of the municipal budget**.
- **Time Banks**: **Time banks** (bourses du temps) allow people to **exchange services** based on **time rather than money**.
- **Local Exchange Trading Systems**: **LETS** (Systèmes d'Échange Local) allow for **local exchange of goods and services** without money.

### French Social Traditions:

- **Mutual Aid**: **Mutual aid** (entraide) is a **long-standing tradition** in France, with **neighbors helping each other**.
- **Neighborhood Festivals**: **Fêtes de quartier** (neighborhood festivals) are **common** and help **build community**.
- **Community Gardens**: **Jardins partagés** (shared gardens) are **popular** in many French cities.
- **Free Stores**: **Donneries** or **recycleries** are **places where people can give and take items for free**.
- **Food Sharing**: **Food sharing initiatives** help **reduce food waste** and **provide meals to those in need**.

### Challenges in French Social Life:

- **Individualism**: There is a **tension between individualism** and **community values** in French society.
- **Urban Anonymity**: In **large cities**, there can be a **sense of anonymity** and **lack of community**.
- **Generational Divide**: There are **differences in values and priorities** between **different generations**.
- **Rural Depopulation**: **Rural areas** are **experiencing depopulation**, leading to **declining services and amenities**.
- **Gentrification**: **Gentrification** in cities is **displacing long-time residents** and **changing neighborhood characters**.
