# B1 Unit 01: Travel Experiences and Adventures

## Learning Objectives
- Describe past travel experiences using past tenses
- Talk about different types of vacations and trips
- Use the past tense (passé composé) with reflexive verbs
- Use the imperfect tense (imparfait) for descriptions
- Discuss travel preferences and experiences
- Understand and use travel-related vocabulary in context
- Talk about cultural experiences while traveling

## Dialogue: Sharing Travel Memories

**Context**: Sophie and her friends are sharing stories about their recent travels.

---

**Sophie**: Alors, qu'est-ce que vous avez fait pendant vos vacances?
**Marie**: Moi, je suis allée en Italie avec ma famille. Nous avons visité Rome, Florence et Venise.

**Sophie**: Waouh! C'était comment?
**Marie**: C'était incroyable! Les monuments étaient magnifiques. Nous avons mangé de la pizza tous les jours.

**Thomas**: Moi, je suis parti en randonnée dans les Alpes. J'ai marché pendant une semaine.
**Sophie**: Tu as campé?

**Thomas**: Oui, j'ai dormi dans une tente tous les soirs. C'était une expérience unique!
**Marie**: Et toi, Sophie? Tu es partie quelque part?

**Sophie**: Oui, je suis allée à Barcelone avec mon petit ami. Nous avons vu la Sagrada Família et nous avons mangé des tapas délicieuses.
**Thomas**: Ça a l'air super! Vous avez pris beaucoup de photos?

**Sophie**: Oui, des centaines! Et vous?
**Marie**: Moi, j'ai pris au moins mille photos! Mon mari dit que je suis obsédée par la photographie.

**Thomas**: Moi, je préférais profiter du moment sans prendre trop de photos. Mais j'ai quand même quelques souvenirs.
**Sophie**: C'est vrai que parfois on passe plus de temps à prendre des photos qu'à regarder autour de soi.

## Dialogue: Planning a Future Trip

**Context**: Paul and his colleague are discussing their dream destinations.

---

**Paul**: Si tu pouvais aller n'importe où dans le monde, où est-ce que tu irais?
**Collègue**: Oh, c'est une bonne question... Je pense que j'irais au Japon. J'ai toujours voulu voir Tokyo et Kyoto.

**Paul**: Moi, je rêve d'aller en Australie. J'aimerais voir la Grande Barrière de corail.
**Collègue**: Ah, c'est sur ma liste aussi! Et qu'est-ce que tu aimerais faire là-bas?

**Paul**: Je voudrais faire de la plongée sous-marine pour voir les poissons tropicaux. Et toi?
**Collègue**: Moi, je voudrais goûter la cuisine japonaise. J'ai entendu dire que les sushis sont incroyables.

**Paul**: Tu as déjà voyagé beaucoup?
**Collègue**: Oui, j'ai été en Europe plusieurs fois, et j'ai aussi visité le Mexique l'année dernière.

**Paul**: C'est impressionnant! Moi, je n'ai été qu'en France et en Espagne jusqu'à présent.
**Collègue**: Il y a tellement d'endroits à voir! J'aimerais visiter tous les continents un jour.

**Paul**: Moi aussi! Mais pour l'instant, je me contente de rêver et d'économiser.

## Grammar: Past Tenses

### Passé Composé (Compound Past)

The **passé composé** is used to describe **completed actions** in the past. It's formed with **avoir** or **être** + past participle.

**With Avoir (most verbs):**

| Subject | Avoir | Past Participle | Example |
|---------|-------|-----------------|---------|
| je | ai | mangé | J'ai mangé (I ate) |
| tu | as | mangé | Tu as mangé (You ate) |
| il/elle/on | a | mangé | Il a mangé (He ate) |
| nous | avons | mangé | Nous avons mangé (We ate) |
| vous | avez | mangé | Vous avez mangé (You ate) |
| ils/elles | ont | mangé | Ils ont mangé (They ate) |

**Common Verbs with Avoir:**
- **manger** → mangé (to eat)
- **boire** → bu (to drink)
- **prendre** → pris (to take)
- **faire** → fait (to do/make)
- **voir** → vu (to see)
- **visiter** → visité (to visit)
- **acheter** → acheté (to buy)
- **prendre des photos** → pris des photos (to take photos)

**Examples:**
- J'**ai visité** Paris l'année dernière. (I visited Paris last year.)
- Tu **as pris** beaucoup de photos. (You took many photos.)
- Il **a mangé** dans un restaurant italien. (He ate in an Italian restaurant.)
- Nous **avons découvert** un nouveau musée. (We discovered a new museum.)

### Passé Composé with Être (Reflexive Verbs and Movement)

Verbs that describe **movement** or **change of state** use **être** in the passé composé. These include:

1. **Reflexive verbs** (verbs with **se**):
   - **se lever** → levé (to get up)
   - **se laver** → lavé (to wash oneself)
   - **se reposer** → reposé (to rest)
   - **s'appeler** → appelé (to be called)

2. **Movement verbs** (DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP):
   - **aller** → allé (to go)
   - **venir** → venu (to come)
   - **partir** → parti (to leave)
   - **arriver** → arrivé (to arrive)
   - **entrer** → entré (to enter)
   - **sortir** → sorti (to go out)
   - **monter** → monté (to go up)
   - **descendre** → descendu (to go down)
   - **tomber** → tombé (to fall)
   - **retourner** → retourné (to return)
   - **passer** → passé (to pass)
   - **rester** → resté (to stay)
   - **mourir** → mort (to die)

**Conjugation with Être:**

| Subject | Être | Past Participle | Example |
|---------|------|-----------------|---------|
| je | suis | allé(e) | Je suis allé(e) (I went) |
| tu | es | allé(e) | Tu es allé(e) (You went) |
| il | est | allé | Il est allé (He went) |
| elle | est | allée | Elle est allée (She went) |
| on | est | allé | On est allé (One went) |
| nous | sommes | allé(e)s | Nous sommes allé(e)s (We went) |
| vous | êtes | allé(e)(s) | Vous êtes allé(e)(s) (You went) |
| ils | sont | allés | Ils sont allés (They went - masc.) |
| elles | sont | allées | Elles sont allées (They went - fem.) |

**Important**: With **être**, the past participle **agrees in gender and number** with the subject:
- Je suis **allé** (I went - masculine)
- Je suis **allée** (I went - feminine)
- Nous sommes **allés** (We went - masculine/mixed)
- Nous sommes **allées** (We went - feminine)

**Examples:**
- Je **suis allé** à Paris. (I went to Paris - masculine speaker)
- Elle **est partie** en vacances. (She left on vacation.)
- Nous **sommes arrivés** à l'hôtel. (We arrived at the hotel - masculine/mixed)
- Elles **sont parties** en voyage. (They left on a trip - feminine)

### Imparfait (Imperfect Tense)

The **imparfait** is used to describe:
1. **Ongoing actions** in the past (what was happening)
2. **Descriptions** in the past (what something was like)
3. **Habitual actions** in the past (what used to happen)
4. **Background information** in a story

**Formation**: Take the **nous form** of the present tense, remove **-ons**, and add the imparfait endings:

| Subject | Ending | Example (parler) |
|---------|--------|-------------------|
| je | -ais | je parl**ais** |
| tu | -ais | tu parl**ais** |
| il/elle/on | -ait | il/elle/on parl**ait** |
| nous | -ions | nous parl**ions** |
| vous | -iez | vous parl**iez** |
| ils/elles | -aient | ils/elles parl**aient** |

**Examples:**
- Je **parlais** français. (I was speaking French. / I used to speak French.)
- Tu **mangeais** beaucoup. (You were eating a lot. / You used to eat a lot.)
- Il **pleuvait**. (It was raining.)
- Nous **étions** fatigués. (We were tired.)
- Vous **aviez** un chien. (You had a dog.)
- Ils **voyageaient** souvent. (They were traveling often. / They used to travel often.)

**Irregular Stems in Imparfait:**
- **être** → ét- (j'étais, tu étais, il était, nous étions, vous étiez, ils étaient)

### Passé Composé vs. Imparfait

| Passé Composé | Imparfait |
|----------------|-----------|
| **Completed actions** | **Ongoing actions** |
| Je **ai mangé** (I ate) | Je **mangeais** (I was eating) |
| **Specific time** mentioned | **No specific time** mentioned |
| **Series of completed actions** | **Background description** |
| **Main action** in a story | **Setting the scene** |

**Examples:**
- **Passé Composé**: Hier, j'**ai visité** le Louvre. (Yesterday, I visited the Louvre.)
- **Imparfait**: Quand j'**étais** à Paris, j'**allais** souvent au Louvre. (When I was in Paris, I often went to the Louvre.)

- **Passé Composé**: J'**ai vu** un accident. (I saw an accident.)
- **Imparfait**: Il **pleuvait** quand j'**arrivais**. (It was raining when I arrived.)

**Combined Example:**
- Quand je **marchais** dans la rue (imparfait - ongoing action), j'**ai vu** un ami (passé composé - completed action).
  (When I was walking down the street, I saw a friend.)

### Reflexive Verbs in the Past

Reflexive verbs use **être** in the passé composé and the past participle agrees with the subject:

**Common Reflexive Verbs:**
- **se lever** → levé (to get up)
- **se laver** → lavé (to wash oneself)
- **se reposer** → reposé (to rest)
- **se baigner** → baigné (to swim/bathe)
- **s'habiller** → habillé (to get dressed)
- **se préparer** → préparé (to get ready)
- **s'amuser** → amusé (to have fun)
- **se promener** → promené (to take a walk)

**Conjugation:**
- Je **me suis lavé(e)** (I washed myself)
- Tu **t'es lavé(e)** (You washed yourself)
- Il **s'est lavé** (He washed himself)
- Elle **s'est lavée** (She washed herself)
- Nous **nous sommes lavé(e)s** (We washed ourselves)
- Vous **vous êtes lavé(e)(s)** (You washed yourselves)
- Ils **se sont lavés** (They washed themselves - masc.)
- Elles **se sont lavées** (They washed themselves - fem.)

**Examples:**
- Je **me suis réveillé(e)** tôt. (I woke up early.)
- Elle **s'est préparée** pour le voyage. (She got ready for the trip.)
- Nous **nous sommes reposés** après la randonnée. (We rested after the hike.)

## Cultural Note: Travel Culture in France

The French have a **rich travel culture** and are known for their **love of exploration** and **appreciation of different cultures**. Here's an overview of travel in French culture:

### The French and Vacation:

- **Paid Vacation**: French workers have **at least 5 weeks of paid vacation** per year (25 days minimum by law, often more with company benefits).
- **Summer Vacation**: The **most popular time** for vacations is **July and August**, when many businesses close and families go on extended trips.
- **School Vacation**: French students have **three main vacation periods**: **Summer** (July-August), **Christmas** (2 weeks), and **Winter/Spring** (2 weeks in February/March).
- **"Les Grandes Vacances"**: The **summer vacation** is the most important, often lasting **6-8 weeks** for students.

### Popular French Vacation Destinations:

**Within France:**
- **Côte d'Azur** (French Riviera): Famous for its **beaches, luxury resorts, and Mediterranean climate** (Nice, Cannes, Saint-Tropez).
- **Paris**: The **most visited city** in the world, known for its **monuments, museums, and culture**.
- **Brittany**: Known for its **rugged coastline, Celtic heritage, and seafood**.
- **Provence**: Famous for its **lavender fields, olive groves, and Roman ruins**.
- **Alsace**: Known for its **half-timbered houses, wine, and Christmas markets**.
- **The Alps**: Popular for **skiing in winter** and **hiking in summer** (Chamonix, Courchevel).
- **The Pyrenees**: Known for **hiking, thermal spas, and the pilgrimage to Lourdes**.
- **Loire Valley**: Famous for its **castles (châteaux)** and **wine**.
- **Burgundy**: Known for its **wine, food, and medieval towns**.
- **Normandy**: Famous for its **D-Day beaches, Mont Saint-Michel, and cheese**.

**International Destinations:**
- **Spain**: The **most popular international destination** for French travelers (close, affordable, great beaches).
- **Italy**: Popular for its **culture, food, and historical sites**.
- **Portugal**: Known for its **beaches, affordable prices, and friendly people**.
- **Morocco**: Popular for its **exotic culture, markets, and desert landscapes**.
- **Tunisia**: Known for its **beaches, Roman ruins, and Sahara desert**.
- **United States**: Popular for **road trips, cities like New York, and national parks**.
- **Canada**: Known for its **natural beauty, Quebec's French culture, and outdoor activities**.
- **Thailand**: Popular for its **beaches, temples, and affordable luxury**.
- **Japan**: Growing in popularity for its **unique culture, food, and technology**.

### French Travel Habits:

- **Last-Minute Bookings**: Many French people **book vacations last-minute**, especially for **domestic travel**.
- **Rental Properties**: **Gîtes** (vacation rentals) are **very popular**, offering **more space and authenticity** than hotels.
- **Camping**: France has **over 10,000 campsites**, and **camping is very popular**, especially in summer.
- **Bed and Breakfasts**: **Chambres d'hôtes** (B&Bs) are **widely available** and offer **local hospitality**.
- **House Swapping**: Some French people **swap houses** with others for vacations.

### French Travel Traditions:

- **"Le Tour de France"**: While primarily a **bicycle race**, many French people **follow the route** for vacations.
- **"Les Soldes"**: The **bi-annual sales** (January and July) are a **popular time for shopping trips**.
- **Christmas Markets**: In **December**, many French people visit **Christmas markets** in Alsace, Germany, or Austria.
- **Festivals**: France has **many festivals** that attract travelers: **Cannes Film Festival, Avignon Theatre Festival, Nice Jazz Festival, etc.**
- **Pilgrimages**: **Lourdes** is one of the **most visited Catholic pilgrimage sites** in the world.

### Travel by Region:

Different regions of France have **distinct travel cultures**:

- **Paris**: **City breaks, cultural tourism, shopping**
- **Provence**: **Rural tourism, wine tasting, lavender fields**
- **Brittany**: **Coastal tourism, seafood, Celtic heritage**
- **Alsace**: **Wine tourism, Christmas markets, half-timbered houses**
- **The Alps**: **Ski tourism, mountain sports, hiking**
- **The Pyrenees**: **Hiking, thermal spas, pilgrimages**
- **Loire Valley**: **Cultural tourism, castle visits, wine tasting**
- **Normandy**: **Historical tourism, D-Day beaches, cheese routes**

### French Travel Etiquette:

- **Greetings**: Always greet people with **"Bonjour"** when entering shops, hotels, or restaurants.
- **Tipping**: **Service charge is included** in bills, but it's **customary to leave small change** (1-2 euros) for good service.
- **Dress Code**: **Dress neatly** when visiting churches or religious sites (cover shoulders and knees).
- **Queueing**: **Respect queues** (lines) - cutting in line is considered **rude**.
- **Punctuality**: For **tours and appointments**, be **punctual**. For **social gatherings**, being **15-30 minutes late** is often acceptable.
- **Language**: **Learn a few basic phrases** in the local language when traveling abroad.

### French Travel Innovations:

- **TGV**: The **high-speed train** (Train à Grande Vitesse) connects **major French cities** at speeds up to **320 km/h**.
- **BlaBlaCar**: A **popular carpooling app** that connects drivers with empty seats to passengers.
- **SNCF**: The **French national railway company** offers **extensive rail network** throughout France and Europe.
- **Vélib'**: **Bike-sharing system** in Paris and other cities.
- **Autolib'**: **Electric car-sharing system** (now replaced by other services).

### Travel in French Literature and Film:

- **"Le Petit Prince"** by Antoine de Saint-Exupéry - A **philosophical tale** about a prince who travels from planet to planet.
- **"Around the World in 80 Days"** (Le Tour du monde en 80 jours) by Jules Verne - A **classic adventure novel**.
- **"The Adventures of Tintin"** by Hergé - **Comic book series** featuring travel and adventure.
- **"Amélie"** (Le Fabuleux Destin d'Amélie Poulain) - A film that **showcases Paris** through the eyes of a shy waitress.
- **"The Intouchables"** (Intouchables) - A film that **explores Paris** through the friendship of two men from different backgrounds.

### Travel Tips for France:

- **Learn Basic French**: Even a few phrases will **enhance your experience** and be **appreciated by locals**.
- **Use Public Transportation**: France has an **excellent public transportation system** (metro, buses, trains).
- **Try Local Specialties**: Each region has its **own culinary specialties** - don't miss out!
- **Respect Local Customs**: **Observe and follow** local customs and traditions.
- **Be Patient**: **Service may be slower** than in some countries, but it's often **more personal**.
- **Explore Beyond Paris**: While Paris is **wonderful**, France has **so much more to offer** beyond the capital.
- **Travel Off-Season**: **Avoid July and August** if possible - it's **crowded and expensive**.
- **Book in Advance**: For **popular attractions**, **book tickets online** to avoid long lines.

### French Travel Proverbs:

- **"Voyager, c'est vivre deux fois."** - "To travel is to live twice."
- **"Le voyage est le meilleur des livres."** - "Travel is the best of books."
- **"Qui voyage beaucoup sait beaucoup."** - "He who travels a lot knows a lot."
- **"Un voyage de mille lieues commence par un pas."** - "A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step." (French version of Chinese proverb)
- **"Le monde est un livre, et ceux qui ne voyagent pas n'en lisent qu'une page."** - "The world is a book, and those who do not travel read only one page." (Saint Augustine)
