# A1 Unit 06: Food and Drinks

## Learning Objectives
- Learn vocabulary for common foods and drinks
- Talk about likes and dislikes
- Order food in a restaurant or café
- Use the verb "vouloir" (to want)
- Use the verb "prendre" (to take/have)
- Understand a simple menu

## Dialogue: At the Café

**Context**: Sophie and Marie are at a café in Paris.

---

**Serveur**: Bonjour, mesdames! Qu'est-ce que vous voulez?
**Marie**: Bonjour! Je veux un café, s'il vous plaît.
**Sophie**: Moi, je veux un thé.

**Serveur**: Vous voulez quelque chose à manger?
**Marie**: Oui, je veux un croissant.
**Sophie**: Et moi, je veux un pain au chocolat.

**Serveur**: Très bien. Et pour finir?
**Marie**: Deux verres d'eau, s'il vous plaît.
**Sophie**: Non, pour moi, un jus d'orange.

**Serveur**: D'accord. Je vous apporte ça tout de suite.
**Marie**: Merci!

**Serveur**: (quelques minutes plus tard) Voici vos boissons et vos viennoiseries.
**Sophie**: C'est délicieux! Merci.

**Marie**: Alors, Sophie, tu aimes le café français?
**Sophie**: Oui, j'adore! Et toi, tu aimes les croissants?
**Marie**: Bien sûr! J'adore la pâtisserie française.

## Dialogue: At the Restaurant

**Context**: Sophie and Marie are having dinner at a restaurant.

---

**Serveur**: Bonsoir, mesdames! Voici la carte. Qu'est-ce que vous prenez?
**Marie**: Bonsoir! Je prends une salade, s'il vous plaît.
**Sophie**: Moi, je prends une soupe.

**Serveur**: Et comme plat principal?
**Marie**: Je prends le poulet avec des légumes.
**Sophie**: Moi, je prends le poisson avec du riz.

**Serveur**: Très bien. Et à boire?
**Marie**: Une bouteille d'eau, s'il vous plaît.
**Sophie**: Et un verre de vin rouge pour moi.

**Serveur**: Parfait. Je prends votre commande.

**Serveur**: (plus tard) Voici vos entrées.
**Marie**: C'est très bon!

**Serveur**: (après le plat principal) Vous voulez un dessert?
**Sophie**: Oui, je veux une glace.
**Marie**: Moi, je veux une tarte aux pommes.

**Serveur**: Très bien. Je vous apporte ça tout de suite.

## Grammar: The Verb "Vouloir" (To Want)

### Present Tense of "Vouloir"

| Subject | French | Pronunciation |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| I | je veux | /zhe vuh/ |
| You (singular) | tu veux | /too vuh/ |
| He/She/It | il/elle veut | /eel/ell vuh/ |
| We | nous voulons | /noo voo-lon/ |
| You (plural) | vous voulez | /voo voo-lay/ |
| They | ils/elles veulent | /eel/ell vuhl/ |

**Examples:**
- Je veux un café. (I want a coffee.)
- Tu veux un thé? (Do you want tea?)
- Il veut une salade. (He wants a salad.)
- Nous voulons des croissants. (We want some croissants.)
- Vous voulez quelque chose à manger? (Do you want something to eat?)
- Ils veulent du vin. (They want some wine.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Negative Form**: "ne...pas" surrounds the verb
   - Je ne veux pas de café. (I don't want any coffee.)
   - Il ne veut pas de dessert. (He doesn't want any dessert.)

2. **Question Form**: Invert subject and verb (or use est-ce que)
   - Veux-tu un café? (Do you want a coffee?)
   - Est-ce que tu veux un thé? (Do you want tea?)

3. **Common Expressions**:
   - Je veux... (I want...)
   - Qu'est-ce que tu veux? (What do you want?)
   - Qu'est-ce que vous voulez? (What do you want? - formal)
   - Je ne veux rien. (I don't want anything.)
   - Je veux tout! (I want everything!)

### Present Tense of "Prendre"

| Subject | French | Pronunciation |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| I | je prends | /zhe pron/ |
| You (singular) | tu prends | /too pron/ |
| He/She/It | il/elle prend | /eel/ell pron/ |
| We | nous prenons | /noo pruh-non/ |
| You (plural) | vous prenez | /voo pruh-nay/ |
| They | ils/elles prennent | /eel/ell pronn/ |

**Examples:**
- Je prends un café. (I'm having a coffee.)
- Tu prends un croissant? (Are you having a croissant?)
- Il prend une salade. (He's having a salad.)
- Nous prenons du vin. (We're having some wine.)
- Vous prenez quelque chose? (Are you having something?)
- Ils prennent le bus. (They're taking the bus.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Common Expressions**:
   - Je prends... (I'm having/taking...)
   - Qu'est-ce que tu prends? (What are you having?)
   - Qu'est-ce que vous prenez? (What are you having? - formal)

2. **In a Restaurant**:
   - Je prends le poulet. (I'll have the chicken.)
   - Je prends une salade et un café. (I'll have a salad and a coffee.)

## Grammar: Partitive Articles

### "De" vs. "Du/De la/Des"

When talking about food and drinks, we often use partitive articles to indicate an unspecified quantity:

| French | English | Pronunciation |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| du | some (masculine) | /doo/ |
| de la | some (feminine) | /duh la/ |
| des | some (plural) | /day/ |
| de l' | some (before vowel) | /duh luh/ |

**Examples:**
- Je veux **du** pain. (I want some bread.)
- Je prends **de la** salade. (I'm having some salad.)
- Il y a **des** légumes. (There are some vegetables.)
- Je bois **de l'** eau. (I'm drinking some water.)

### Negative Form

In negative sentences, the partitive article changes to **"de"** or **"d'"**:

- Je ne veux pas **de** pain. (I don't want any bread.)
- Je ne prends pas **de** salade. (I'm not having any salad.)
- Il n'y a pas **d'** eau. (There is no water.)
- Je ne bois pas **de** vin. (I'm not drinking any wine.)

## Cultural Note: French Food Culture

Food is a central part of French culture and daily life. Here are some important aspects:

**Meal Times**:
- **Petit déjeuner** (breakfast): Usually between 7:00 and 9:00 AM. Typically includes bread, pastries, coffee, or hot chocolate.
- **Déjeuner** (lunch): The main meal of the day, usually between 12:00 and 2:00 PM. Many people have a full meal with multiple courses.
- **Goûter** (afternoon snack): Around 4:00 PM, especially for children. Often includes a pastry or bread with chocolate.
- **Dîner** (dinner): Usually between 7:00 and 9:00 PM. Often lighter than lunch but can be a full meal.

**Bread Culture**:
- Bread (pain) is a staple of French cuisine. The baguette is the most iconic French bread.
- Bakeries (boulangeries) are found in every neighborhood.
- It's common to buy fresh bread daily.
- Bread is often served with meals and used to soak up sauces.

**Café Culture**:
- Cafés are an important part of French social life.
- People often meet at cafés for coffee, drinks, or light meals.
- It's common to sit at a café for a long time, reading, talking, or people-watching.
- Espresso (expresso) is the most common coffee order.
- Café crème is coffee with cream, similar to a latte.

**Restaurant Culture**:
- Meals at restaurants often last longer than in some other countries.
- It's common to have multiple courses: appetizer (entrée), main course (plat principal), cheese, and dessert.
- Bread is usually served with the meal at no extra charge.
- Tipping is not as expected as in some other countries, but it's polite to leave a small tip (5-10%).
- Service charge is often included in the bill (service compris).

**Regional Specialties**:
- France has a rich culinary diversity with regional specialties:
  - **Brittany**: Crêpes, galettes, seafood
  - **Normandy**: Camembert cheese, apples, cider
  - **Burgundy**: Beef bourguignon, escargots, wine
  - **Lyon**: Sausages, poultry, quiche
  - **Provence**: Ratatouille, bouillabaisse, herbs
  - **Alsace**: Sauerkraut, tarte flambée, wine

**Cheese Culture**:
- France is famous for its cheese, with over 1,000 varieties.
- Cheese is often served after the main course and before dessert.
- Common cheeses include Brie, Camembert, Roquefort, Comté, and many others.
- Each region has its own specialty cheeses.

**Wine Culture**:
- Wine (vin) is an important part of French culture and meals.
- France is one of the world's largest wine producers.
- Wine is often served with meals, even on weekdays.
- Common types include red (rouge), white (blanc), and rosé.
- Champagne is a sparkling wine from the Champagne region.

**Market Culture**:
- Outdoor markets (marchés) are common in France.
- People often buy fresh produce, cheese, meat, and other goods at markets.
- Markets are usually held on specific days of the week.
- It's common to bargain slightly at some markets.
