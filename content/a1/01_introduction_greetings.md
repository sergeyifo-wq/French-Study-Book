## Grammar: Basic Sentence Structure

### Subject Pronouns

In French, subject pronouns are used before the verb to indicate who is performing the action.

### Negative Form

To make a sentence negative, place **ne...pas** around the verb:

- Je **ne** suis **pas** Marie. (I am not Marie.)
- Tu **n'**es **pas** Paul. (You are not Paul.)
- Il **n'**est **pas** franais. (He is not French.)

**Note**: In spoken French, **ne** is often dropped, but it's important to include it in writing.

### Questions with Intonation

In French, you can often form questions simply by changing your intonation:

- Vous **tes** franais. (You are French.) → Vous **tes** franais**?** (Are you French?)
- Tu **es** Marie. (You are Marie.) → Tu **es** Marie**?** (Are you Marie?)

### Questions with "Est-ce que"

A more formal way to ask questions is to use **Est-ce que** at the beginning:

- **Est-ce que** vous tes franais? (Are you French?)
- **Est-ce que** tu es Marie? (Are you Marie?)
- **Est-ce que** vous parlez anglais? (Do you speak English?)

## Cultural Note: Greetings in French Culture

In France, greetings are **very important** and follow specific customs:

**Formal vs. Informal:**
- **Formal greetings** (Bonjour, Comment vous appelez?) are used with **strangers, elders, and people in positions of authority** (teachers, bosses, etc.).
- **Informal greetings** (Salut, Comment tu t'appelles?) are used with **friends, family, and peers**.
- When in doubt, **start with formal greetings** and switch to informal if the other person suggests it.

**The Importance of "Bonjour":**
- In France, it's **essential** to greet people with **Bonjour** when entering any establishment (shops, restaurants, offices, etc.).
- **Not greeting someone** can be seen as **rude or impolite**.
- This applies even in **brief interactions**, like asking for directions or making a quick purchase.

**Cheek Kissing (La Bise):**
- Among **friends and family**, the French often greet each other with **cheek kisses** (la bise).
- The **number of kisses** varies by region: **2 kisses** (most common), **3 kisses** (in some regions), or **4 kisses** (in southern France).
- **Men** typically **shake hands** with other men, but may give **cheek kisses** to women they know well.
- **Always follow the other person's lead** - if they offer their cheek, reciprocate.

**Handshakes:**
- In **formal situations** and **business settings**, a **firm handshake** is appropriate.
- Maintain **eye contact** during the handshake.
- Handshakes are also used when **meeting someone for the first time** in a formal context.

**Greetings Throughout the Day:**
- **Bonjour** is used from **morning until evening** (roughly until 6 PM).
- **Bonsoir** is used from **evening onwards** (after 6 PM).
- **Salut** can be used at any time of day, but only in **informal situations**.

**Parting Phrases:**
- **Au revoir** is the most common way to say goodbye in **formal situations**.
- **Salut** can be used to say goodbye in **informal situations**.
- **a bientt** (See you soon) and **a plus tard** (See you later) are used when you expect to see the person again soon.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**
- **Don't use "Salut" with strangers** - it can seem too casual or even rude.
- **Don't skip greetings** when entering a shop or restaurant.
- **Don't use first names** unless invited to do so in formal situations.
- **Don't be afraid to ask** if you're unsure about the appropriate level of formality.

**Regional Variations:**
- In **southern France**, people may be **more likely to use cheek kisses**, even in slightly more formal situations.
- In **northern France**, handshakes may be **more common** in situations where southerners might use cheek kisses.
- In **Paris**, the customs are generally **more formal** than in other parts of the country.

**Tips for Learners:**
- **Observe** how French people greet each other in different situations.
- **Start with formal greetings** and adjust based on the other person's response.
- **Don't be offended** if someone seems more formal than you expected - it's often a sign of respect.
- **Practice** with friends or language partners to get comfortable with different types of greetings.
- **Remember**: A **smile and polite greeting** will always be appreciated, even if your pronunciation isn't perfect!
