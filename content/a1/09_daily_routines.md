## Dialogue: A Day in the Life

**Context**: Marie describes her daily routine to Sophie.

---

**Sophie**: Marie, qu'est-ce que tu fais habituellement pendant la journée?
**Marie**: Je me réveille à 7 heures. Ensuite, je me lève et je vais dans la salle de bain.

**Sophie**: Tu prends une douche le matin?
**Marie**: Oui, je me douche et je me brosse les dents. Après, je m'habille.

**Sophie**: Et pour le petit déjeuner?
**Marie**: Je prends un café et un croissant. Parfois, je mange des céréales.

**Sophie**: À quelle heure est-ce que tu vas au travail?
**Marie**: Je pars de la maison à 8 heures. Je prends le bus pour aller au bureau.

**Sophie**: Tu travailles jusqu'à quelle heure?
**Marie**: Je travaille jusqu'à 17 heures. Après le travail, je rentre à la maison.

**Sophie**: Qu'est-ce que tu fais le soir?
**Marie**: Je prépare le dîner, je regarde la télévision ou je lis un livre. Je me couche à 23 heures.

**Sophie**: Et le week-end, tu fais la même chose?
**Marie**: Non, le week-end je me lève plus tard, vers 9 heures. Je fais les courses et je vois mes amis.

---

## Grammar: Reflexive Verbs

### What Are Reflexive Verbs?

Reflexive verbs are verbs where the subject and the object are the same. In other words, the person doing the action is also receiving the action.

In French, reflexive verbs always use a reflexive pronoun:
- **me** (myself)
- **te** (yourself)
- **se** (himself, herself, itself, oneself)
- **nous** (ourselves)
- **vous** (yourself/yourselves)
- **se** (themselves)

### Present Tense of Reflexive Verbs

The reflexive pronoun comes BEFORE the verb in the present tense:

| Subject | Reflexive Pronoun | Verb | Example |
|---------|-------------------|------|---------|
| Je | me | lave | Je me lave (I wash myself) |
| Tu | te | lèves | Tu te lèves (You get up) |
| Il/Elle/On | se | lave | Il se lave (He washes himself) |
| Nous | nous | lavons | Nous nous lavons (We wash ourselves) |
| Vous | vous | lavez | Vous vous lavez (You wash yourself) |
| Ils/Elles | se | lavent | Ils se lavent (They wash themselves) |

**Examples:**
- Je **me** réveille à 7 heures. (I wake up at 7 o'clock.)
- Tu **te** lèves tôt. (You get up early.)
- Il **se** lave les mains. (He washes his hands.)
- Nous **nous** habillons. (We get dressed.)
- Vous **vous** reposez. (You rest.)
- Ils **se** couchent tard. (They go to bed late.)

### Important Notes:

1. **Placement**: The reflexive pronoun comes BEFORE the verb in simple tenses (present, future, etc.)
   - Je **me** lave. (I wash myself.)
   - Tu **te** lèves. (You get up.)

2. **Negative Form**: "ne...pas" surrounds the reflexive pronoun AND the verb
   - Je **ne me** lave **pas**. (I don't wash myself.)
   - Il **ne se** lave **pas** les mains. (He doesn't wash his hands.)

3. **Question Form**: Invert subject and verb, keeping the reflexive pronoun before the verb
   - **Te** lèves-tu tôt? (Do you get up early?)
   - **Se** lave-t-il les mains? (Does he wash his hands?)

4. **With Body Parts**: When using reflexive verbs with body parts, use the definite article (le, la, les)
   - Je me lave **les** mains. (I wash **my** hands.)
   - Il se brosse **les** dents. (He brushes **his** teeth.)
   - Elle se lave **le** visage. (She washes **her** face.)

## Cultural Note: Daily Life in France

Daily routines in France reflect the culture's emphasis on work-life balance, enjoyment of life, and social connections. Here are some insights into daily life in France:

**Morning Routine**:
- The French typically wake up between 7:00 and 8:00 AM on weekdays.
- Breakfast (petit déjeuner) is usually light, often consisting of bread or pastries with coffee or hot chocolate.
- Many people read the newspaper or listen to the radio during breakfast.
- The French often take their time in the morning, enjoying their coffee and breakfast.

**Work Culture**:
- The legal work week is 35 hours, with many people working from Monday to Friday.
- Lunch break (pause déjeuner) is typically 1-2 hours, and many people go home for lunch.
- It's common to have a long lunch break, especially in smaller towns.
- The French value their lunch break as a time to relax and enjoy a proper meal.

**Lunch**:
- Lunch (déjeuner) is often the main meal of the day.
- Many businesses and schools have a long lunch break, from around 12:00 to 2:00 PM.
- It's common to have multiple courses for lunch: appetizer, main course, cheese, and dessert.
- Wine or water is typically served with lunch.
- Many people socialize with colleagues or friends during lunch.

**Afternoon**:
- After lunch, many people return to work or school.
- The afternoon work period is typically from 2:00 to 5:00 or 6:00 PM.
- It's common to have a short break in the afternoon (goûter) for a snack or coffee.
- Many shops close for a few hours in the afternoon, especially in smaller towns.

**Evening**:
- Dinner (dîner) is usually between 7:00 and 9:00 PM.
- Dinner is often a social event, with family or friends.
- The French typically eat later than in some other countries.
- Dinner often includes multiple courses and can last for several hours.
- After dinner, it's common to relax, watch TV, read, or socialize.

**Bedtime**:
- The French typically go to bed between 10:00 PM and midnight.
- Bedtime routines often include reading, watching TV, or listening to music.
- Many people enjoy a nightcap (un digestif) before bed.

**Weekends**:
- Weekends are for relaxation, family time, and socializing.
- Saturday is often a day for shopping, errands, and social activities.
- Sunday is typically a day for family, rest, and leisure activities.
- Many people take long walks or hikes on weekends.
- Sunday lunch is often a special family meal.

**"La Pause Café"**:
- The French often take coffee breaks throughout the day.
- A morning coffee break (pause café du matin) is common.
- An afternoon coffee break (pause café de l'après-midi) is also typical.
- Coffee breaks are social events, often with colleagues or friends.

**"L'Apéritif"**:
- The apéritif is a pre-dinner drink with snacks, typically between 6:00 and 8:00 PM.
- It's a social event, often with friends or family.
- Common apéritif drinks include wine, champagne, or cocktails.
- Snacks (amuse-bouches) are often served with the drinks.

**"La Sieste"**:
- In some parts of France, especially in the south, people take a short nap after lunch (la sieste).
- This is less common in cities and among younger people.
- The sieste is typically 20-30 minutes long.

**Public Holidays**:
- France has many public holidays (jours fériés), with most businesses closing.
- Common holidays include New Year's Day, Easter, May Day, Bastille Day (July 14), and Christmas.
- Many French people take advantage of holidays for short trips or family gatherings.

**Vacations**:
- The French typically have 5 weeks of paid vacation per year.
- August is a popular month for vacations, with many businesses closing.
- Many French people take long vacations in the summer.
- Popular vacation destinations include the beach, the mountains, and the countryside.

**Social Life**:
- The French value social connections and often spend time with friends and family.
- Meals are often social events, with long conversations and enjoyment of good food and wine.
- It's common to invite friends over for dinner or drinks.
- The French often celebrate birthdays and other special occasions with meals and gatherings.
