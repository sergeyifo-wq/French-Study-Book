# A1 Unit 04: Family and Friends

## Learning Objectives
- Learn vocabulary for family members
- Describe relationships and family structure
- Ask and answer questions about family
- Use possessive adjectives
- Talk about friends and social relationships

## Dialogue: Meeting the Family

**Context**: Marie introduces her family to her friend Sophie.

---

**Marie**: Sophie, je te présente ma famille. Voici mon père, Pierre.
**Sophie**: Enchantée, Monsieur! Je m'appelle Sophie.
**Pierre**: Enchanté, Sophie! Bienvenue dans notre maison.

**Marie**: Et voici ma mère, Claire.
**Sophie**: Bonjour, Madame! Votre maison est très belle.
**Claire**: Merci, Sophie! C'est gentil.

**Marie**: J'ai aussi un frère, Thomas. Il a 25 ans.
**Thomas**: Salut, Sophie! Je suis le frère de Marie.
**Sophie**: Salut, Thomas! Tu es plus jeune que Marie?
**Thomas**: Oui, j'ai 25 ans et Marie a 28 ans.

**Marie**: Et voici ma sœur, Élodie. Elle est étudiante.
**Élodie**: Bonjour, Sophie! Je suis la sœur cadette de Marie.
**Sophie**: Bonjour, Élodie! Vous êtes tous très sympas.

**Marie**: Et toi, Sophie? Tu as des frères ou des sœurs?
**Sophie**: J'ai un frère. Il s'appelle Paul. Et j'ai beaucoup d'amis!

---

## Grammar: Possessive Adjectives

### My, Your, His/Her/Its

In French, possessive adjectives agree in gender and number with the thing being possessed, NOT the possessor:

| Person | Singular (Masculine) | Singular (Feminine) | Plural |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------|-------|
| I | mon | ma | mes |
| You (singular) | ton | ta | tes |
| He/She/It | son | sa | ses |
| We | notre | notre | nos |
| You (plural) | votre | votre | vos |
| They | leur | leur | leurs |

**Examples:**
- mon père (my father) - masculine singular
- ma mère (my mother) - feminine singular
- mes parents (my parents) - plural
- ton frère (your brother) - masculine singular
- ta sœur (your sister) - feminine singular
- son ami (his/her friend) - masculine singular
- sa maison (his/her house) - feminine singular
- ses enfants (his/her children) - plural

### Important Notes:

1. **Gender Agreement**: The possessive adjective agrees with the NOUN, not the owner
   - mon livre (my book - masculine)
   - ma voiture (my car - feminine)
   - mes livres (my books - plural)

2. **Before Vowels**: Use the masculine form before feminine words that start with a vowel
   - mon amie (my friend - feminine word starting with vowel)
   - mon école (my school - feminine word starting with vowel)

3. **Plural**: All possessive adjectives have the same plural form (mes, tes, ses, nos, vos, leurs)

## Cultural Note: Family in France

In France, family is very important, but the concept of family can be different from other cultures:

**Nuclear Family**: The immediate family (parents and children) is the most common household structure. Extended families often live separately but maintain close relationships.

**Family Size**: French families have been getting smaller over the years. The average French family has 1-2 children, but larger families (3-4 children) are not uncommon.

**Family Names**: In France, children traditionally take their father's last name. However, since 2005, parents can choose to give their children either the father's name, the mother's name, or both (hyphenated).

**Family Gatherings**: Large family gatherings are common for special occasions like birthdays, holidays (especially Christmas and Easter), and weddings. Sunday lunches with the extended family are also a tradition in many French households.

**Generational Relationships**: Grandparents often play an active role in their grandchildren's lives. It's common for grandparents to help with childcare or to have regular contact with their grandchildren.

**Marriage and Partnerships**: In France, there are different types of recognized partnerships:
- Marriage (mariage)
- Civil union (PACS - Pacte Civil de Solidarité)
- Cohabitation (concubinage)

The PACS was introduced in 1999 and provides many of the legal rights of marriage without the formal commitment.

**Single Parent Families**: France has a relatively high rate of single-parent families, and there's strong social support for single parents.
